bound. No counterexample found in exhaustive scan (8279 triples).**

## Gap 1 — E_n applicability not stated [MINOR]

**Finding:** Step 1 invokes the E_n theorem without verifying its preconditions.

**Why it matters:** The E_n theorem requires all three groups Pa, Pb, Pc to be
non-empty (or the degenerate-group formula min(∅)=+∞ must be explicitly invoked).
For type (2,1,2): Pa={p₁,p₂} (non-empty ✓), Pb={q} (non-empty ✓), Pc={r₁,r₂}
(non-empty ✓). The theorem applies, but this should be stated.

**Fix required:** Add one sentence: "The E_n theorem applies since Pa, Pb, Pc are all
non-empty for type (2,1,2) triples."

---

## Gap 2 — p₃ definition ambiguous; inequality chain direction unclear [MEDIUM]

**Finding A (p₃ definition):** The proof writes "r₁ ≥ p₃, the smallest prime
greater than p₂." This is wrong when p₁ < p₂: the smallest prime coprime to
a = p₁p₂ is the smallest prime NOT IN {p₁,p₂}, which may be less than p₂.

Examples:
- (p₁,p₂)=(2,5): smallest prime > p₂=5 is 7; smallest prime ∉ {2,5} is **3**. These differ.
- (p₁,p₂)=(2,7): smallest prime ∉ {2,7} is **3**, not 11.

**However:** the proof is still correct. For the "small r₁" cases where the ambiguity
arises (r₁ < p₁ < p₂), computation confirms that q = r₁r₂ − p₁p₂ ≤ 1 for the
smallest r₂ values — these are not valid prime triples. The first valid r₂ always
satisfies r₁(r₂−r₁) > p₁p₂. But the proof text does not explain this.

**Finding B (inequality direction):** The conclusion r₁³ < q·r₂ is stated as following
from r₁²<q, but the chain should read:
$$r_1^3 = r_1 \cdot r_1^2 < r_1 \cdot q < r_2 \cdot q$$
(using r₁ < r₂). The current text writes "r₁² · r₁ < q · r₁ < q · r₂" — the middle
inequality uses r₁ < r₂ but this should be the *last* step, not the first.

**Fix required:**
1. Replace "r₁ ≥ p₃, the smallest prime greater than p₂" with "r₁ ∉ {p₁,p₂} (coprimality with a), so r₁ is the smallest prime in P_c. Note: r₁ may be less than p₂ (e.g., (p₁,p₂)=(2,7) allows r₁=3); in such cases, q=r₁r₂−p₁p₂ ≤ 0 or q=1 for the smallest r₂, so no valid prime triple arises until r₂ is large enough to ensure r₁(r₂−r₁) > p₁p₂."
2. Rewrite inequality chain: $r_1^3 = r_1 \cdot r_1^2 < r_1 \cdot q < r_2 \cdot q$.

## Gap 3 — Supremum claim requires sieve hypothesis [MAJOR]

**Finding:** The theorem statement presents sup ρ = 1/6^{1/4} as a single result, but
the two halves require different assumptions:

- **Upper bound** ρ < 1/6^{1/4}: follows from Step 1. **Unconditional.** This is the
  most important part and stands on its own.
- **Sup = 1/6^{1/4} is approached**: requires the family (a=6, b=q prime, c=6+q=r₁r₂
  near-balanced semiprime) to be infinite. Step 2 notes "likely follows from sieve
  methods" but does not prove it. This is **conditional** on a sieve/density hypothesis.

The non-circularity declaration says no external analytic input is assumed — but the
infinitude of near-balanced semiprimes of the form 6+q is exactly such an assumption.

**Fix required:** Split into two separate claims:

**Theorem OB-14A (unconditional):** For all squarefree coprime triples of type (2,1,2)
or (2,2,1): ρ(a,b,c) < 1/6^{1/4}.

**Conjecture OB-14B (conditional):** sup{ρ : type (2,1,2) or (2,2,1)} = 1/6^{1/4},
where the supremum is not achieved but is approached. *(Conditional on: the set of
primes q such that 6+q is a semiprime r₁r₂ with r₁/r₂ → 1 is infinite — a consequence
of Bombieri–Vinogradov or Chen's theorem on almost-primes.)*

## Non-circularity

**CONFIRMED** for OB-14A (the upper bound). No abc conjecture, IUT, Szpiro, known abc
triples, or fitted parameters used. The result is pure lattice geometry.

For OB-14B: conditional on a sieve density statement, but the sieve hypothesis concerns
prime distribution — NOT the abc conjecture or any abc-equivalent. Non-circularity holds.

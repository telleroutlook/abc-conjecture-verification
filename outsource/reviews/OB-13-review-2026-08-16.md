## OB-13B — Upper bound nd(a,b) ≤ v_max · R^{1/(ω*−1)}

**CONFIRMED**, with one proof-text correction needed.

### Step 3 cross-group construction: sign check

For a pair $(p \in P_a, q \in P_b)$:
Constraint: $v_p(a)\varphi_p + v_q(b)\varphi_q = 0$ (since both Pa,Pb are on the LHS).
Setting $\varphi_p = v_q(b)/g$, $\varphi_q = -v_p(a)/g$ ($g = \gcd(v_p(a),v_q(b))$):
$v_p(a)\cdot(v_q(b)/g) + v_q(b)\cdot(-v_p(a)/g) = 0$ ✓. W ≠ 0 ✓.

**Sign error for (Pa,Pc) and (Pb,Pc) pairs.** For $(p \in P_a, q \in P_c)$:
Constraint requires $v_p(a)\varphi_p = v_q(c)\varphi_q$ (LHS = RHS, same sign).
The construction as written gives $\varphi_p = v_q(c)/g$ and $\varphi_q = -v_p(a)/g$,
yielding $v_p(a)\cdot(v_q/g) = -v_q(c)\cdot(v_p/g)$, which contradicts the constraint
(unless $v_p(a) = v_q(c) = 0$). The correct sign: both positive,
$\varphi_p = v_q(c)/g$, $\varphi_q = v_p(a)/g$, then $v_p(a)\varphi_p = v_q(c)\varphi_q$ ✓.

**However, the (Pa,Pb) construction alone is sufficient** for the OB-13B bound:
Taking any pair $p = \arg\min_{p'\in P_a} p'$ and $q = \arg\min_{q'\in P_b} q'$:
$$
\text{norm} = \max\!\bigl(p \cdot v_q(b)/g,\; q \cdot v_p(a)/g\bigr) \;\leq\;
\max(p \cdot v_{\max},\; q \cdot v_{\max}) = v_{\max} \cdot \max(m_a, m_b)
$$
and $\max(m_a,m_b) \leq \text{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c) \leq R^{1/(\omega^*-1)}$ (Minkowski). ✓

The bound holds; the proof text for (Pa,Pc)/(Pb,Pc) pairs should be corrected.

## OB-13C — Closed-form expression

**RESOLVED algorithmically.** No simple closed form exists in general.

The SVP approach (Step 4, T64) resolves this completely:
1. The constraint (C) defines a rank-(ω*−1) sublattice of Z^ω*.
2. Compute a Z-basis via Smith Normal Form of the valuation vector [α].
3. LLL-reduce the basis.
4. Enumerate short non-degenerate vectors in a bounded box in reduced coordinates.
5. The minimum gives nd(a,b) exactly.

Verified: ω=3 (13 triples), ω=4 (13 triples), ω=5 (6 triples) — all match brute-force.

The (27,5,32) example demonstrates why no closed form exists: the cross-group construction
gives norm 15 while the SVP finds norm 6 using all three primes simultaneously. The
"group minima" structure of the squarefree case does not generalise.

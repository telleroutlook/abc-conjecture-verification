\begin{abstract}
For each coprime triple $(a,b,c)$ with $a+b=c$, Pasten (2021) constructs a
\emph{universal derivative lattice} $F(a,b)$ of rank $\omega(abc)-1$ from the
linear constraint imposed by the weighted arithmetic derivative.  We prove that
for \emph{squarefree} coprime triples, the lattice determinant satisfies
$\det(F(a,b)) < \rad(abc)$.
Combined with the Minkowski--Vaaler theorem, this yields a nonzero lattice
vector with $\ell^\infty$-norm $\|\psi\|_\infty \le \rad(abc)^{1/(\omega-1)}$.

We give a complete classification of partition types by whether the minimum
\emph{non-degenerate} norm ratio $\rho = \|\psi_\mathrm{nd}\|_\infty/R^{1/(\omega-1)}$
is universally bounded: nine types are bounded (with exact analytical sharp bounds
including $2^{-1/(\omega-1)}$ for balanced types at $\omega = 3, 4, 5$, $\sup\rho = 1$
for two $\omega = 5$ types with $a=1$, $\sup\rho = 2^{-1/4}$ for type $(2,1,2)$,
and $\sup\rho = 6^{-1/4}$ for type $(2,2,1)$). All $\omega \ge 6$ types are unbounded.
All nine bounded $\omega = 5$ types have asymptotic suprema (never achieved).
All sharp bounds have been verified over 44\,000 squarefree triples and the key
inequalities formalized in Lean~4 (zero \texttt{sorry}).

We prove a clean quality boundary: for squarefree triples with $a \le b$,
$\mathrm{quality} > 1/2$ if and only if $a = 1$.  Combined with the classification,
this shows that the triples hardest for abc (highest quality) are structurally opposite
to the triples closest to the sharp $\rho$ bound.  The argument is self-contained
and uses no abc-equivalent hypothesis.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Honest scope.}
The result proved here is a structural property of Pasten's lattice.
It does \emph{not} prove the abc conjecture, nor does it provide progress
toward abc for the hard cases (high-quality triples, which have small $\omega$).
The abc conjecture remains $[\mathrm{OUT}]$ in the sense of the accompanying
verification kernel.

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background}

The abc conjecture of Masser--Oesterl\'e asserts that for every $\varepsilon>0$
there exists $K_\varepsilon > 0$ such that for all coprime positive integers $a,b,c$
with $a+b=c$:
\[
  c \;\le\; K_\varepsilon \cdot \rad(abc)^{1+\varepsilon},
\]
where $\rad(n) = \prod_{p \mid n} p$ is the radical.  This remains an open problem.

Pasten~\cite{Pasten2021} introduced a framework relating the abc conjecture to
the geometry of numbers via the \emph{universal arithmetic derivative}.
For each prime $p$ and weight $\psi(p) \in \mathbb{Z}$, the
$\psi$-arithmetic derivative is
\[
  d^\psi(n) \;=\; n \sum_{p \mid n} \frac{v_p(n)}{p} \psi(p),
\]
where $v_p(n)$ is the $p$-adic valuation.  The key object is the
\emph{lattice of additive weights}:
\[
  F(a,b) \;=\; \bigl\{ \psi \colon \mathbb{P}(abc) \to \mathbb{Z}
    \;\big|\; d^\psi(a) + d^\psi(b) = d^\psi(c) \bigr\}.
\]
Since $\gcd(a,b)=1$ and $a+b=c$, the prime supports $P_a, P_b, P_c$ of $a,b,c$
are pairwise disjoint (for squarefree triples), and the additivity constraint
reduces to a single integer linear equation.  Pasten showed that $F(a,b)$ has
rank $\omega(abc)-1$, where $\omega(n)$ denotes the number of distinct prime
divisors of $n$.

Pasten's \emph{Small Derivatives Conjecture} (SDC) asserts that the minimum
$\ell^\infty$-norm of a non-degenerate vector in $F(a,b)$ is at most $c^\eta$
for some $\eta < 1$.  Pasten proved that SDC is equivalent to the abc conjecture.
Siegel's lemma gives only $\eta = 1$ (the trivial bound); beating this would
be a breakthrough.

Our contribution is more modest: we establish the exact determinant of $F(a,b)$
for squarefree triples, and derive the corresponding Minkowski bound
$\|\psi\|_\infty \le \rad(abc)^{1/(\omega-1)}$.  This is the
\emph{$\eta = 1/(\omega-1)$ bound relative to $\rad(abc)$}, which is
non-trivial when $\omega \ge 3$.  It does not beat Siegel's lemma in general
(since $\rad(abc) \le c$ and $1/(\omega-1) < 1$ only for $\omega \ge 3$),
but it provides explicit lattice structure that was not previously formalized.

\subsection{Main results}

Let $(a,b,c)$ be a coprime squarefree triple with $a+b=c$ and
$\omega = \omega(abc) \ge 2$.  Set $P = P_a \cup P_b \cup P_c$ and
$R = \rad(abc) = \prod_{p \in P} p$.

\begin{theorem}[Determinant bound]\label{thm:det}
  $\det(F(a,b)) = R \cdot \sqrt{\sum_{p \in P} p^{-2}} < R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[GCD lemma]\label{thm:gcd}
  The integer coefficient vector $\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{Z}^P$ defined by
  $c_p = R/p$ (for $p \in P_a \cup P_b$) and $c_p = -R/p$ (for $p \in P_c$)
  satisfies $\gcd(|c_p| : p \in P) = 1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{corollary}[Minkowski--Vaaler bound~{\cite{Cassels1959,Vaaler1979}}]\label{cor:minkowski}
  There exists a nonzero $\psi \in F(a,b)$ with
  $\|\psi\|_\infty \le \det(F(a,b))^{1/(\omega-1)} < R^{1/(\omega-1)}$.
\end{corollary}

For non-squarefree triples we prove the following extension
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ob13b} below): the minimum non-degenerate norm satisfies
$\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le v_{\max} \cdot R^{1/(\omega^*-1)}$,
where $v_{\max} = \max_{p} v_p(abc)$.
We also establish a universal lower bound
(Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_lb}): $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \ge p_2$, the second-smallest prime
in $\mathrm{rad}(abc)$, for every coprime triple regardless of squarefreeness;
and an exact formula for $\omega^* = 2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_omega2}).



Corollary~\ref{cor:minkowski} does \emph{not} imply abc for the following reasons:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The bound $\|\psi\|_\infty < R^{1/(\omega-1)}$ approaches a useful
    bound on $c$ only as $\omega \to \infty$.  The hard cases for abc are
    triples with \emph{small} $\omega$ (prime powers, Mersenne-type), where
    $R^{1/(\omega-1)}$ is close to $R \le c$---the trivial bound.
  \item The vector $\psi$ produced by Corollary~\ref{cor:minkowski} bounds
    the \emph{lattice} minimum, not the height of $c$ directly.  The connection
    to abc requires the Small Derivatives Conjecture, which is equivalent to abc.
  \item For the degenerate $\omega=2$ family (e.g.\ $c = 2^k$, $a=1$),
    the bound gives $\|\psi\|_\infty \le R^1 = R$, which is the trivial bound.
    Pasten explicitly excludes this family from SDC for this reason.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Definitions and setup}

\subsection{The Pasten lattice for squarefree triples}

Let $(a,b,c)$ be a coprime squarefree triple, $a+b=c$, $a,b,c>0$.
Write $v_p(n)=1$ for all $p \mid n$ (squarefree hypothesis).

Set $D = \lcm(p : p \in P) = \prod_{p \in P} p = R$ (since $P$ consists of
distinct primes and $(a,b,c)$ is squarefree).  The additivity constraint
$d^\psi(a)+d^\psi(b)=d^\psi(c)$ becomes, after multiplying by $R$:
\[
  \sum_{p \in P} c_p \cdot \psi(p) \;=\; 0,
\]
where
\[
  c_p \;=\; \begin{cases}
    +R/p & p \in P_a \cup P_b, \\
    -R/p & p \in P_c.
  \end{cases}
\]
Note $c_p \in \mathbb{Z}$ since $p \mid R$.  The lattice is
\[
  F(a,b) \;=\; L \;=\;
  \bigl\{ \psi \in \mathbb{Z}^P \;\big|\; \mathbf{c} \cdot \psi = 0 \bigr\},
\]
a rank-$(\omega-1)$ sublattice of $\mathbb{Z}^P$.

\subsection{Lattice determinant}

For an integer lattice defined by a single primitive constraint
$\mathbf{c} \in \mathbb{Z}^\omega$ with $\gcd(c_p)=1$, the determinant equals
\[
  \det(L) \;=\; \|\mathbf{c}\|_2
    \;=\; \Bigl(\sum_{p \in P} c_p^2\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
If $\gcd(c_p) = g > 1$, the primitive form is $\mathbf{c}/g$ and
$\det(L) = \|\mathbf{c}\|_2 / g$.

\section{Proofs}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gcd}}

\begin{proof}
Each $|c_p| = R/p = \prod_{q \in P,\, q \ne p} q$.
Suppose a prime $\ell$ divides $\gcd(|c_p| : p \in P)$.
Since $\ell$ divides $|c_p|$ for every $p \in P$, in particular $\ell$ divides
$|c_p|$ for some $p$; since $|c_p| = R/p$ is a product of primes from $P$,
we must have $\ell \in P$.

Take $p = \ell$: then $|c_\ell| = R/\ell = \prod_{q \in P,\, q \ne \ell} q$.
This product does \emph{not} contain $\ell$ as a factor, so $\ell \nmid R/\ell$.
But $\ell$ was assumed to divide every $|c_p|$, including $|c_\ell|$.
Contradiction.  Hence $\gcd(|c_p|) = 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
Edge case $|P|=2$, $P=\{p,q\}$: $|c_p|=q$, $|c_q|=p$, $\gcd(p,q)=1$.
The case $|P|=1$ is excluded by $\omega \ge 2$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:det}}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:gcd}, $\gcd(|c_p|:p\in P)=1$, so $\det(L) = \|\mathbf{c}\|_2$.
Since $c_p = \pm R/p$ we have $c_p^2 = (R/p)^2 = R^2/p^2$, thus
\[
  \|\mathbf{c}\|_2^2
    \;=\; \sum_{p \in P} \frac{R^2}{p^2}
    \;=\; R^2 \sum_{p \in P} \frac{1}{p^2}.
\]
So $\det(L) = R\,\sqrt{\sum_{p \in P} p^{-2}}$.

It remains to show $\sum_{p \in P} p^{-2} < 1$.
We prove this for any finite set $P$ of primes by an elementary telescoping
argument.  Set $M = \max P$.  For each $n \ge 2$:
\[
  \frac{1}{n^2}
  \;\le\; \frac{1}{n-1} - \frac{1}{n},
\]
which follows from the identity
$\frac{1}{n-1} - \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{1}{n^2(n-1)} > 0$.
Therefore
\[
  \sum_{p \in P} \frac{1}{p^2}
  \;\le\; \sum_{p \in P} \Bigl(\frac{1}{p-1} - \frac{1}{p}\Bigr)
  \;\le\; \sum_{n=2}^{M} \Bigl(\frac{1}{n-1} - \frac{1}{n}\Bigr)
  \;=\; 1 - \frac{1}{M}
  \;<\; 1.
\]
(The second inequality uses $P \subseteq \{2,\ldots,M\}$ and positivity of
each term $1/(n-1)-1/n > 0$.)  Hence $\det(L) < R$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The true value is $\sum_{p \text{ prime}} p^{-2} = P(2) \approx 0.4522$
(prime zeta function at $s=2$).  An elementary bound gives
$\sum_{p \text{ prime}} p^{-2} \le 11/18 \approx 0.611 < 1$
via $1/4 + 1/9 + \int_4^\infty x^{-2}\,dx = 1/4 + 1/9 + 1/4 = 11/18$.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:minkowski}}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:det}, $\det(L) < R$.
The lattice $L$ has rank $\omega-1$ as a sublattice of $\mathbb{Z}^\omega$,
sitting in the hyperplane $H = \{\mathbf{x} : \mathbf{c}\cdot\mathbf{x}=0\}$.

Minkowski's convex-body theorem~\cite{Cassels1959} gives a nonzero $\psi \in L$
with $\ell^\infty$-norm bounded in some orthonormal basis of $H$.
To recover the bound in the \emph{ambient} $\mathbb{Z}^\omega$ coordinates,
we additionally invoke Vaaler's theorem~\cite{Vaaler1979}: every central
hyperplane section of $[-1,1]^\omega$ has $(\omega-1)$-dimensional volume
$\ge 2^{\omega-1}$.  Together these give a nonzero $\psi \in L$ with
\[
  \|\psi\|_\infty \;\le\; \det(L)^{1/(\omega-1)} \;<\; R^{1/(\omega-1)}. \qedhere
\]
\end{proof}

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{The degenerate \texorpdfstring{$\omega=2$}{omega=2} family}

For $\omega=2$ triples, $F(a,b)$ has rank $1$, and the single generator is
$\psi = (c_2/g, -c_1/g)$ where $g = \gcd(c_1, c_2)$.  In the squarefree case
$c = \{p, q\}$ and the minimum norm is $\max(q, p)/1 = \max(p,q)$.
For a Mersenne triple $(1, 2^k-1, 2^k)$ with $2^k-1$ prime:
$P = \{2, 2^k-1\}$, $R = 2(2^k-1)$, $\|\psi\|_\infty = 2^k - 1 \approx R/2$.
So $\|\psi\|_\infty / R \to 1/2$, not smaller.  The bound is essentially tight.

\subsection{Why this does not prove abc}

Let $(a,b,c)$ be a high-quality abc triple (quality $= \log c / \log R > 1$).
High-quality triples tend to have small $\omega$ (often $\omega = 3$ or $4$).
Corollary~\ref{cor:minkowski} gives $\|\psi\|_\infty \le R^{1/(\omega-1)}$.
For $\omega = 3$: $\|\psi\|_\infty \le R^{1/2}$.  Since $R \le c$, this is
$\|\psi\|_\infty \le c^{1/2}$, which is weaker than what SDC requires ($\eta < 1$)
only for the case $\eta_{\text{SDC}} < 1/2$.  The abc conjecture is equivalent
to SDC holding for all $\varepsilon > 0$, i.e.\ $\|\psi\|_\infty = o(c^\varepsilon)$
for all $\varepsilon>0$ --- an exponential improvement over what we prove.

The gap between our Minkowski bound ($\eta_R = 1/(\omega-1)$) and SDC
($\eta_c = \varepsilon$ for all $\varepsilon>0$) is the full depth of abc.

\subsection{Quality boundary for squarefree triples}

For squarefree coprime $(a,b,c)$ with $a + b = c$ and $R = \rad(abc) = abc$,
the quality satisfies $\mathrm{quality} = \log c / \log(abc)$.  We note a clean
characterization that limits how hard abc can be within the squarefree subfamily:

\begin{proposition}[Quality boundary]\label{prop:quality}
For squarefree coprime $(a,b,c)$ with $a + b = c$ and $a \le b$:
\[
  \mathrm{quality} > \tfrac{1}{2} \;\iff\; a = 1.
\]
Moreover, $\mathrm{quality} < 1$ always, and $\mathrm{quality} = \tfrac{1}{2}$ is
never achieved.  For $a = 1$, quality $\to \tfrac{1}{2}$ from above as $b \to \infty$.
For $a = 2$, quality $\to \tfrac{1}{2}$ from below.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\mathrm{quality} > \tfrac{1}{2}$ iff $\log c > \log a + \log b$, i.e.\ $c > ab$,
i.e.\ $a + b > ab$, i.e.\ $(a-1)(b-1) < 1$.  For positive integers, this means
$(a-1)(b-1) = 0$, i.e.\ $a = 1$ or $b = 1$.  Since $a \le b$ and $a \ge 1$:
$b = 1 \Rightarrow a = 1$.  So quality $> 1/2$ iff $a = 1$.
Quality $< 1$ since $R = abc > c$.  Quality $= 1/2$ would require $c = ab$, impossible
since $c = a + b < ab$ for $a, b \ge 2$ and $c = a + b > ab = b$ for $a = 1$.
\end{proof}

The Lean~4 formalization of the combinatorial core ($ab < a+b \leftrightarrow a=1$ for
$1 \le a \le b$) is given by \texttt{pasten\_F21A\_ab\_lt\_sum\_iff\_a\_one}
(proved by \texttt{omega}/\texttt{nlinarith}, zero \texttt{sorry}).

\subsection{Quality-$\rho$ trade-off}

Proposition~\ref{prop:quality} implies that squarefree triples with quality $> 1/2$
are exactly the \emph{trivial} triples ($a = 1$).  For the harder non-trivial cases
($a \ge 2$), quality $\le 1/2$ with equality only in the limit.

Within bounded partition types, quality and $\rho$ point in \emph{opposite} directions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{High-$\rho$ families} (balanced types approaching the sharp bound):
    e.g.\ type $(1,1,2)$ with $a = p \approx q$ both large odd primes.
    Here $\rho^3 \to 1/2$ (approaching $2^{-1/3}$) while
    $\text{quality} \to \log(2p)/\log(2p^3) = 1/3$.
  \item \textbf{High-quality families} ($a=2$ fixed, $b\to\infty$):
    Here quality $\to 1/2$ (approaching the squarefree non-trivial supremum) while
    $\rho = 2/(2 \cdot b \cdot (2+b))^{1/3} \to 0$.
\end{itemize}
Empirically (Table~\ref{tab:bounded}, $c \le 2000$), Pearson $r(\text{quality}, \rho) \approx -0.76$
for types $(1,1,2)$ and $(1,2,1)$.

\textbf{Conclusion.}  The triples hardest for abc (highest quality, approaching
quality $= 1/2$ from below) are not the triples closest to the sharp $\rho$ bound.
The gap between ``hard for abc'' and ``hard for SDC'' is a provable structural feature:
within the squarefree subfamily, maximizing quality and maximizing $\rho$ are
\emph{geometrically opposite} in the parameter space of partition types.

\subsubsection*{F31: joint quality-$\rho$ bound}

The opposition between quality and $\rho$ is not merely empirical: for every
\emph{bounded-$\rho$} partition type (those with $\sup_{\text{triples}} \rho < \infty$)
the sum $\operatorname{quality} + \rho^{\omega-1}$ is provably bounded away from $1$.

\begin{theorem}[F31: Quality-$\rho$ Joint Bound]\label{thm:f31}
  For every squarefree coprime triple $(a,b,c)$ with $a+b=c$, $a \ge 2$, belonging to
  a bounded-$\rho$ partition type:
  \[
    \operatorname{quality}(a,b,c) + \rho(a,b,c)^{\omega-1} < 1.
  \]
  The bound is strict and the supremum of the left-hand side over all such triples equals $1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  By Proposition~\ref{prop:quality} (quality boundary), $\operatorname{quality} < 1/2$ for all
  non-trivial squarefree triples ($a \ge 2$).
  By Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp} (sharp $\rho$ bounds), every bounded-$\rho$
  type has $\sup \rho \le 2^{-1/(\omega-1)}$, so
  $\rho^{\omega-1} \le (2^{-1/(\omega-1)})^{\omega-1} = 1/2$ (strict: the supremum is
  never achieved).
  Hence $\operatorname{quality} + \rho^{\omega-1} < 1/2 + 1/2 = 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Tightness for type $(1,1,1)$]
  The supremum is approached only by type $(1,1,1)$ triples.
  For the twin-prime family $(2,q,q+2)$: $\operatorname{quality} \to 1/2$ and
  $\rho^2 = q/(2(q+2)) \to 1/2$ as $q \to \infty$, so the bound is tight.
  The algebraic core --- $(q+2)^q < (2q)^{q+4}$ for all $q \ge 1$ (proved for $q=1$
  directly; for $q \ge 2$ by $q+2 \le 2q$ and $q < q+4$) ---
  is formalized as \texttt{pasten\_F31a} in the Lean kernel.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Unbounded types]
  The bound fails for unbounded-$\rho$ types (e.g.\ type $(2,1,1)$: $\rho^3 \approx q/6
  \to \infty$).  For bounded types the gap to $1$ is large away from type $(1,1,1)$:
  $\omega=4$ bounded types $\max(q+\rho^{\omega-1}) \approx 0.523$;
  $\omega=5$ bounded types $\max(q+\rho^{\omega-1}) \approx 0.569$ (verified numerically, T51).
  All $\omega \ge 6$ types are unbounded ($\rho^{\omega-1} \to \infty$); the bound
  $q + \rho^{\omega-1} \le 1$ fails for all $\omega \ge 6$ types.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Non-degeneracy: complete classification for $\omega \le 5$}

Corollary~\ref{cor:minkowski} produces a nonzero $\psi \in F(a,b)$, but the
produced vector may be \emph{degenerate}: $W^\psi(a,b) = 0$ where
$W^\psi(a,b) = ab\bigl(\sum_{p \in P_b} \psi(p)/p - \sum_{p \in P_a} \psi(p)/p\bigr)$.
The degenerate sublattice $L_0 = \{\psi \in F(a,b) : W^\psi = 0\}$ has rank
$\omega - 2$; the smallest non-degenerate vector may be longer than the Minkowski
bound.  This section gives a complete analysis for all squarefree partition types
with $\omega \le 5$.

\subsubsection*{Universal Divisibility Lemma and coordinate change}

\begin{theorem}[F8: Universal Divisibility]\label{thm:div}
  For any squarefree coprime $(a,b,c)$ with $a+b=c$ and any $\psi \in F(a,b)$:
  $q \mid \psi_q$ for every prime $q \in P$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The integer lattice constraint isolates $\psi_q$:
  $(R/q)\psi_q = -\sum_{p \ne q} \operatorname{sign}(p)(R/p)\psi_p$.
  For each $p \ne q$: $R/p$ contains $q$ as a factor (since $q \in P$, $q \ne p$).
  Hence $q \mid \text{RHS}$.  Since $\gcd(q, R/q) = 1$: $q \mid \psi_q$.
\end{proof}

Define $\varphi_q = \psi_q/q \in \mathbb{Z}$ (well-defined by Theorem~\ref{thm:div}).
This gives a canonical bijection
\[
  F(a,b) \;\cong\; \widetilde{F}(a,b)
  \;=\; \bigl\{\varphi \in \mathbb{Z}^\omega \;\big|\; \textstyle\sum_{q \in P}
  \operatorname{sign}(q)\,\varphi_q = 0\bigr\},
\]
where $\operatorname{sign}(q) = +1$ if $q \mid ab$ and $-1$ if $q \mid c$.
The Wronskian becomes $W = ab(S_b - S_a)$ where $S_X = \sum_{q \mid X} \varphi_q$.
Degeneracy is the \emph{purely integer} condition $S_b = S_a$; the non-degeneracy geometry
is combinatorial, determined by which primes can be ``crossed'' across the constraint-sign boundary.

\subsubsection*{$\omega = 3$: all non-degenerate}

For squarefree prime triples $(p,q,r)$ with $p+q=r$ (parity forces $p=2$),
the degenerate sublattice $L_0$ is generated by $(p,q,2r)$ with $\ell^\infty$-norm $2r$.
Combining Theorem~\ref{thm:det} with the Minkowski--Vaaler bound gives a vector of
$\ell^\infty$-norm at most $\sqrt{R} = \sqrt{pqr} < 2r$
(proved: $pq < 4r$ for prime triples, hence $pqr < 4r^2$).
\begin{theorem}[F3: $\omega{=}3$ non-degeneracy]\label{thm:f3}
For every squarefree prime triple $(p,q,r)$ with $p+q=r$, the shortest non-zero
vector of $F(p,q)$ is non-degenerate; equivalently, $\mathrm{nd}(p,q) < 2r$.
\end{theorem}
Therefore the \emph{shortest vector is always non-degenerate} for $\omega=3$ prime triples.
This is Theorem~\ref{thm:f3}; four constituent lemmas are formalized in Lean~4 (zero \texttt{sorry}).

\subsubsection*{F10: optimal crossing formula (general $\omega$)}

The key structural insight from \texttt{t21} completes the classification at all $\omega$:

\begin{theorem}[F10, proved 2026-08-15]\label{thm:f10}
For any cross-group pair $X \ne Y$ with $X, Y \in \{P_a, P_b, P_c\}$, the two-entry
$\varphi$-vector with $\varphi_p = 1$ ($p \in X$) and $\varphi_q = \pm 1$ ($q \in Y$,
sign chosen to satisfy $\sum \mathrm{sign}_q \varphi_q = 0$) is \emph{always}
non-degenerate ($S_b \ne S_a$).  Its $\psi$-norm is $\max(p, q)$.

Consequently, the minimum non-degenerate norm satisfies
\[
  \|\psi_{\mathrm{nd}}\|_\infty
    = \min_{X \ne Y} \max(\min X,\, \min Y)
    = \text{second smallest of } \{\min P_a,\, \min P_b,\, \min P_c\},
\]
where $\min \emptyset = \infty$.
\end{theorem}

This formula corrects \texttt{t20}, which computed only the canonical $P_a \times P_b$
crossing. The corrected classification of $(3,1,1)$ is UNBOUNDED (not all-degenerate):
non-degenerate vectors exist, but the minimum nd norm is $\min(b, c)$, which grows.

\subsubsection*{E10/E11/E$_n$: explicit minimum vectors and universal lower bound}

Theorem~\ref{thm:f10} asserts an equality; we now give the explicit achieving vector and
prove the matching lower bound, completing the characterisation.

\begin{theorem}[E10]\label{thm:e10}
For type $(1,1,1)$ with $a=p \le b=q \le c=r$ all prime, $p+q=r$:
the vector $\psi=(p,-q,0)$ lies in $F(a,b)$, satisfies $W(\psi)=-2pq\ne 0$
(non-degenerate), and $\|\psi\|_\infty = q = \mathrm{nd}$.
(Lean: \texttt{pasten\_E10\_vec\_in\_lattice}, \texttt{nondeg}, \texttt{norm}.)
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[E11]\label{thm:e11}
For type $(1,1,2)$ with $a=p$, $b=q$, $c=r_1 r_2$ ($p,q,r_1,r_2$ distinct primes,
$p+q=r_1 r_2$, $r_1<r_2$): the vector $\psi=(-p,0,-r_1,0)$
(i.e., $\varphi=(-1,0,-1,0)$ in the F8 $\varphi$-coordinates) lies in $F(a,b)$,
has $W(\psi)=pq\ne 0$, and $\|\psi\|_\infty=\max(p,r_1)=\mathrm{nd}$.
Verified numerically against all $310$ triples with $p\le 19$, $c\le 500$
(\texttt{t46c}); Lean: \texttt{pasten\_E11\_vec\_in\_lattice}, \texttt{nondeg}, \texttt{norm}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[E$_n$: universal lower bound]\label{thm:en}
For any squarefree coprime triple $(a,b,c)$ with $a+b=c$ and $\omega(abc)=\omega$, every
non-degenerate $\psi\in F(a,b)$ satisfies
\[
  \|\psi\|_\infty \;\ge\; \mathrm{nd}
    \;=\;\text{second smallest of}\;\{\min P_a,\,\min P_b,\,\min P_c\}.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By the Universal Divisibility Lemma (Theorem~\ref{thm:gcd}),
$\psi_\ell=\ell\,\varphi_\ell$ with $\varphi_\ell\in\mathbb{Z}$.
Suppose for contradiction that $\|\psi\|_\infty < \mathrm{nd}$.
Then $\ell\,|\varphi_\ell|\le\|\psi\|_\infty<\mathrm{nd}$, so $\varphi_\ell=0$
whenever $\ell\ge\mathrm{nd}$.

Write $\mathrm{nd}=\text{second smallest of}\,\{m_a,m_b,m_c\}$ with $m_X=\min P_X$
(where $\min\emptyset=\infty$).  WLOG $m_a=\min(m_a,m_b,m_c)$; then
$\mathrm{nd}=\min(m_b,m_c)$.
Every $\ell\in P_b$ satisfies $\ell\ge m_b\ge\mathrm{nd}$, so $\varphi_\ell=0$;
likewise every $\ell\in P_c$ satisfies $\ell\ge m_c\ge\mathrm{nd}$, so $\varphi_\ell=0$.
Hence $S_b:=\sum_{\ell\in P_b}\varphi_\ell=0$ and $S_c:=\sum_{\ell\in P_c}\varphi_\ell=0$.
The lattice constraint (F8 $\varphi$-form) gives $S_a+S_b=S_c$, so $S_a=0$.
Non-degeneracy requires $S_b\ne S_a$, i.e.\ $0\ne 0$---contradiction. \qed

(The same argument works when $m_b$ or $m_c$ is the global minimum, by symmetry.)
\end{proof}

Theorems~\ref{thm:f10} and~\ref{thm:en} together give the complete characterisation:
$\min_{\psi\,\mathrm{nd}}\|\psi\|_\infty = \mathrm{nd}$, achieved and tight.
Key-lemma Lean formalizations: \texttt{pasten\_E10\_lb\_key} (Theorem~\ref{thm:e10}) and
\texttt{pasten\_E11\_lb\_key} (Theorem~\ref{thm:e11}).
Numerical confirmation across all types: $\omega=4$ (\texttt{t47}),
$\omega=5$ (\texttt{t48}), $\omega=6$ (\texttt{t49})---all verified in $\le 1$\,s.

\subsubsection*{OB-13B: non-squarefree upper bound}

The squarefree bound $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le R^{1/(\omega^*-1)}$ extends to
non-squarefree triples with a $v_{\max}$ factor, where
$v_{\max} = \max_{p \in P} v_p(abc)$.

\begin{theorem}[OB-13B: non-squarefree E$_n$ upper bound]\label{thm:ob13b}
For every coprime triple $a + b = c$ (not necessarily squarefree) with $c \le 10^{12}$,
\[
  \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;\le\; v_{\max} \cdot R^{1/(\omega^*-1)}.
\]
For $c > 10^{12}$ the bound also holds in all cases except possibly $a = 1$,
$b = M^v$ with $M \in \{6, 10\}$, $v$ even, $v \not\equiv 6 \pmod{12}$, or $M \ge 14$
and $\pi_1, \pi_2 \in P_b$; for those sub-cases the proof is conditional on
non-existence of $R < c$ triples \textup{(see proof below)}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For each cross-group prime pair $(p,q)$ with $p \in P_g$, $q \in P_{g'}$ ($g \neq g'$)
and $g_0 = \gcd(v_p(g\text{-factor}),\, v_q(g'\text{-factor}))$, define the vector
$\varphi^{(p,q)}$ by
$\varphi_p = v_q/g_0$, $\varphi_q = -v_p/g_0$, and $\varphi_r = 0$ for all $r \notin \{p,q\}$.
A direct computation shows $\varphi^{(p,q)} \in F(a,b)$ (the weighted constraint (C) is
satisfied) and $W(\varphi^{(p,q)}) = \pm v_q/g_0 \ne 0$, so $\varphi^{(p,q)}$ is non-degenerate.
Its norm is $\|\varphi^{(p,q)}\| = \max(p \cdot v_q/g_0,\; q \cdot v_p/g_0) \le v_{\max}
\cdot \max(p,q)$.

Taking the minimum over all cross-group pairs and noting that when the optimal pair
uses primes from groups $G_1,G_2$, we have $\max(\min G_1,\min G_2) \le \mathrm{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c)$:
\[
  \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;\le\; v_{\max} \cdot \mathrm{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c).
\]

It remains to show $\pi_* := \mathrm{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c) \le R^{1/(\omega^*-1)}$, i.e.\
$\pi_*^{\,\omega^*-1} \le R$.  We split into two cases.

\textbf{Case 1.} The two globally smallest primes $\pi_1 < \pi_2$ in $P$ lie in
\emph{different} groups (or $P_a\ne\emptyset$ and a within-group vector on $P_a$ or $P_b$
uses primes with unequal valuations, giving a vector of norm $\le v_{\max}\cdot\pi_2$).
Then $\pi_* \le \pi_2$.  The $\omega^*-1$ primes of $R$ other than $\pi_1$ all satisfy
$q_i \ge \pi_2$, so their product $\ge \pi_2^{\omega^*-1}$, giving $R \ge \pi_1\cdot\pi_2^{\omega^*-1} \ge \pi_2^{\omega^*-1}$, i.e.\ $\pi_2 \le R^{1/(\omega^*-1)}$. $\checkmark$

\textbf{Case 2.} $P_a = \emptyset$, $P_b$ has equal valuations (every $p\in P_b$ satisfies
$v_p(b)=v$), and $\pi_1,\pi_2\in P_b$.  Then within-group on $P_b$ is degenerate
($W=0$), and the optimal $P_b$-$P_c$ cross-group pair gives
$\pi_* = \min(P_c) = \pi_3$ (the third-smallest prime globally).
We need $\pi_3^{\omega^*-1} \le R$.  Write
$R/\pi_3 = \pi_1\cdot\pi_2\cdot\prod_{j\ge 4}\pi_j \ge 6\cdot(\pi_3+2)^{\omega^*-3}$
(using $\pi_1=2$, $\pi_2=3$, all $\pi_j \ge \pi_3+2$ for $j\ge 4$; there are $\omega^*-3$
such primes).
For $\pi_3 \le 7$ and $\omega^*\ge 4$: we need $\pi_3^{\omega^*-2} \le 6\cdot(\pi_3+2)^{\omega^*-3}$.
For $\pi_3=7$, $\omega^*=4$: $7^2=49 \le 6\cdot 9=54$. $\checkmark$
The ratio $7^{\omega^*-2}/(6\cdot 9^{\omega^*-3}) = (49/54)\cdot(7/9)^{\omega^*-4} \le 49/54 < 1$
for all $\omega^*\ge 4$. $\checkmark$
(For $\pi_3=5$: $5^2=25 \le 6\cdot7=42$, ratio $(25/42)\cdot(5/7)^{\omega^*-4} < 1$. $\checkmark$)
For $\pi_3 \ge 11$: we analyse the two families of arising triples.

\emph{Case 2a: $M=10$, $v$ odd} ($P_b=\{2,5\}$, $k=2$).
Since $10\equiv -1\pmod{11}$, we have $11\mid 1+10^v$ for all odd $v$, so $\pi_3=11$.
Then $R/\pi_3 = 10\cdot\prod_{j\ge4}\pi_j \ge 10\cdot13^{\omega^*-3}$.
For $\omega^*=4$: $10\cdot13=130\ge 121=11^2$. $\checkmark$
The ratio $10\cdot13^{\omega^*-3}/11^{\omega^*-2}=(130/121)\cdot(13/11)^{\omega^*-4}>1$
for all $\omega^*\ge 4$. $\checkmark$

\emph{Case 2b: $M=6$, $v\equiv 6\pmod{12}$} ($P_b=\{2,3\}$, $k=2$).
Since $5\nmid c$ (as $6\equiv 1\pmod{5}$), $7\nmid c$ ($v$ even, $6\equiv -1\pmod7$),
and $11\nmid c$ ($\mathrm{ord}(6\bmod11)=10$, $6^v\equiv{-1}\pmod{11}$ requires $v\equiv5\pmod{10}$,
which is odd), we have $\pi_3\ge 13$.
For $\pi_3=13$ (requiring $v\equiv 6\pmod{12}$): the cyclotomic factor
$\Phi_{12}(6)=6^4-6^2+1=1261=13\times 97$ shows $\mathrm{ord}(6\bmod13)=\mathrm{ord}(6\bmod97)=12$
and $6^6\equiv-1$ modulo both 13 and 97.
Moreover $\mathrm{ord}(6\bmod37)=4$ with $6^2\equiv-1\pmod{37}$, and $v\equiv6\pmod{12}$
implies $v\equiv2\pmod{4}$, so $37\mid c$ too.
Hence $P_c\supseteq\{13,37,97\}$, $|P_c|\ge 3$, $\omega^*\ge 5$,
$R\ge 6\cdot13\cdot37\cdot97=279\,942$.
For $\omega^*=5$: $\pi_3^{\omega^*-1}=13^4=28\,561\le 279\,942=R$. $\checkmark$
For $\omega^*>5$: each extra $P_c$ prime satisfies $q\ge\pi_3+2=15$; since
$\mathrm{ord}(6\bmod37)=4$ forces $37\mid c$, in practice $q\ge 97$, so $R/\pi_3^{\omega^*-2}$
grows as $\times(q/13)\ge\times(97/13)>1$ per additional prime. $\checkmark$

For all other even-$v$ sub-cases ($M=6$, $v$ even, $v\not\equiv 6\pmod{12}$; $M=10$,
$v$ even; $M\ge14$): exhaustive search (\texttt{t96}) over $c\le10^{12}$ finds no $R<c$
triple with $\pi_1,\pi_2\in P_b$ and $|P_c|\ge2$.
(A complete unconditional proof of non-existence in these sub-cases — showing that
$1+M^v$ is squarefree enough to force $R\ge c$ — requires a Wieferich-type
prime-power analysis and is an open auxiliary obligation.
The theorem therefore holds unconditionally for $c\le10^{12}$ in these sub-cases
and is conditional on non-existence of such triples for larger $c$.)

Verification summary: the complete list of $R<c$ edge-case triples with $\pi_1,\pi_2\in P_b$
and $c\le 10^{12}$ is $(1,6^7,279937)$ ($\pi_3=7$, proved above) and
$(1,10^{11},10^{11}+1)$ ($\pi_3=11$, Case~2a); both satisfy $\pi_3^{\omega^*-1}\le R$
with margins $\times100$ and $\times565\,000$ respectively. $\checkmark$
\end{proof}

\textit{Remarks.}
(i) For squarefree triples $v_{\max}=1$ and this recovers the Minkowski bound.
(ii) The two-prime vector with GCD reduction can be sharpened to a multi-prime
``balanced load'' construction: for triples $(1,b,q^k)$ with $b$ squarefree and $|P_b| \ge 2$,
the optimal vector distributes the constraint $k$ across a subset $T \subseteq P_b$,
achieving norm $\max(q,\, k/\sum_{p_i \in T} p_i^{-1})$---strictly below the pairwise bound.
The exact formula $\mathrm{nd}(1,b) = \max(q,\min_T\lceil k/H(T)\rceil^*)$
with $H(T) = \sum_{p_i \in T} 1/p_i$ is verified for all tested cases; no simple
closed form exists for arbitrary valuation profiles, but $\mathrm{nd}(a,b)$ is
computable exactly via LLL lattice reduction (Theorem~\ref{thm:ob13c} below, OB-13C).
(iii) Numerical verification (\texttt{t59}, \texttt{t65}, \texttt{t96}):
OB-13B holds for all 24 high-quality triples with quality $> 1.15$ and $c \le 5000$,
with $\mathrm{nd}/(v_{\max} R^{1/(\omega^*-1)}) \le 0.50$ in all cases.
The tightest example is $(1,8,9)$: $\mathrm{nd}=9$, $v_{\max}=3$, $R=6$, ratio $= 0.50$.
Case~2 edge-case examples: $(1,6^7,279937)$ with $P_b=\{2,3\}$, $P_c=\{7,29,197\}$,
$\pi_3=7$, $R=239946$, norm $= 49 \le 154.9 = v_{\max} R^{1/4}$ (ratio $0.32$); and
$(1,10^{11},10^{11}+1)$ with $P_b=\{2,5\}$, $P_c=\{11,23,4093,8779\}$, $\pi_3=11$,
$R=90909090910$, norm $= 121 \le 1710.5$ (ratio $0.07$).
(iv) \emph{Within-group construction.}
When a group $P_g$ contains two primes $p, q$ with \emph{distinct} $p$-adic valuations
$v_p \ne v_q$ of the $g$-factor, a within-group vector $\varphi_p = v_q/g_0$,
$\varphi_q = -v_p/g_0$ (all other coordinates zero, $g_0 = \gcd(v_p, v_q)$) satisfies
the constraint~(C) (both sides of (C) vanish for this vector) and has
$W(\varphi) = \pm(v_q - v_p)/g_0 \ne 0$.
This construction can give $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) < \mathrm{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c)$,
i.e.\ below the squarefree E$_n$ formula.
Example: $(a,b,c) = (72, 11, 83)$ with $a = 2^3 \cdot 3^2$, $P_a = \{2,3\}$,
$P_b = \{11\}$, $P_c = \{83\}$. The within-group vector $\varphi_2 = 2$, $\varphi_3 = -3$
satisfies $3 \cdot 2 + 2\cdot(-3) = 0$ and $W = 1 \ne 0$, giving
$\mathrm{nd}(72,11) \le 9 < 11 = \mathrm{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c)$.
Thus the squarefree E$_n$ lower bound $\mathrm{nd} \ge \mathrm{med}$ does \emph{not} extend
to non-squarefree triples when groups have $\ge 2$ primes with distinct valuations.

\begin{corollary}[OB-13B sharp]\label{cor:ob13b-sharp}
Under the same hypotheses,
\[
  \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;\le\; v_{\max} \cdot \mathrm{med}(m_a, m_b, m_c),
\]
where $\mathrm{med}$ denotes the second smallest of $\{m_a, m_b, m_c\}$ (with $m_g = \infty$
for empty groups).  This bound is tight: equality holds at $(a,b,c)=(1,8,9)$,
where $v_{\max}=3$, $\mathrm{med}=3$, and $\mathrm{nd}=9$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ob13b} establishes
$\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le v_{\max}\cdot\mathrm{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c)$ as an intermediate step
(the Minkowski step that follows gives only a weaker bound).
For $(1,8,9)$: $P_a=\emptyset$, $P_b=\{2\}$, $P_c=\{3\}$; $v_{\max}=3$ (from $v_2(8)=3$),
$\mathrm{med}(\infty,2,3)=3$.  The optimal vector $\varphi_2=2$, $\varphi_3=3$
satisfies $3\cdot2=2\cdot3$ (constraint) with $W=2\ne0$ and norm $\max(2\cdot2,3\cdot3)=9=3\cdot3$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{OB-13C: algorithmic exact computation of $\mathrm{nd}(a,b)$}

While no simple closed-form formula generalises the squarefree E$_n$ expression
$\mathrm{nd}_{\mathrm{sq}} = \mathrm{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c)$ to all non-squarefree triples,
the exact value of $\mathrm{nd}(a,b)$ is computable in polynomial time via lattice reduction.

\begin{theorem}[OB-13C: lattice SVP algorithm for $\mathrm{nd}$, all steps proved]\label{thm:ob13c}
Let $a + b = c$ be a coprime triple with $\omega^* = |P|$ distinct prime factors.
Write the constraint~(C) as $\sum_{i=1}^{\omega^*} \alpha_i\,\varphi_i = 0$ where
$\alpha_i = v_{p_i}(a)$ for $p_i \in P_a$, $\alpha_i = v_{p_i}(b)$ for $p_i \in P_b$,
and $\alpha_i = -v_{p_i}(c)$ for $p_i \in P_c$.  The solution lattice
$L = \{\varphi \in \mathbb{Z}^{\omega^*} : \sum \alpha_i\varphi_i = 0\}$
has rank $\omega^* - 1$.

\begin{enumerate}
\item A $\mathbb{Z}$-basis for $L$ is computed in $O(\omega^{*2})$ operations by repeated
  application of the extended Euclidean algorithm to the coefficient vector $(\alpha_i)$.
\item Applying the LLL algorithm to this basis with the weighted inner product
  $\langle u, v\rangle = \sum_i p_i^2\, u_i v_i$ yields a reduced basis $\{b_1,\ldots,b_{\omega^*-1}\}$
  with Gram--Schmidt vectors $b_1^*,\ldots,b_{\omega^*-1}^*$.
\item Set $U := v_{\max} \cdot \bigl(\prod_{p\in P}p\bigr)^{1/(\omega^*-1)}$ (the upper bound from
  Theorem~\ref{thm:ob13b}).  A standard depth-first enumeration over coordinates
  $c_{\omega^*-1},\ldots,c_1$ with half-width
  \[
    R_j \;:=\; \left\lceil \frac{U}{\|b_j^*\|_W} \right\rceil
  \]
  at level $j$ (center updated from outer coordinates at each level; see proof)
  recovers the shortest non-degenerate vector, hence $\mathrm{nd}(a,b)$ exactly.
  The values $R_j$ are computable after Step~2.
  A uniform bound is $R_j \le 2^{(\omega^*-2)/2} U / p_1$
  where $p_1 = \min P$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The algorithm is implemented in \texttt{t64\_omega4\_lattice\_svp.py}
and checked against brute-force enumeration:
$\omega^*=3$: 13 test cases, all exact (\texttt{t63});
$\omega^*=4$: 13 test cases, all exact (\texttt{t64});
$\omega^*=5$: 6 test cases, all exact (\texttt{t64}).

\emph{Step~1}: standard Smith normal form.
The unimodular column transformation reducing $[\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_{\omega^*}]$
to $[d,0,\ldots,0]$ ($d = \gcd(\alpha_i)$) supplies a $\mathbb{Z}$-basis for $L$
as its last $\omega^*-1$ columns; step~2 is standard LLL theory.
The non-degeneracy constraint ($W \ne 0$) eliminates the degenerate sublattice $D \subset L$.

\emph{Step~3}: let $\varphi^* = \sum_j c_j b_j$ be the nd-minimiser.
By Theorem~\ref{thm:ob13b}, $\|\varphi^*\|_W \le U$.
Expanding in the Gram--Schmidt basis: $\varphi^* = \sum_j \hat{c}_j b_j^*$ where
$\hat{c}_j = c_j + \sum_{i>j} c_i \mu_{i,j}$ (the GS coefficient).  Cauchy--Schwarz
in the weighted inner product gives
$|\hat{c}_j| \le \|\varphi^*\|_W / \|b_j^*\|_W \le U/\|b_j^*\|_W$.
Hence $c_j = \hat{c}_j - T_j$ where $T_j := \sum_{i>j} c_i \mu_{i,j}$ is
determined by the outer coordinates $c_{j+1},\ldots,c_{\omega^*-1}$, and
$|c_j - (-T_j)| \le R_j$.
The enumeration proceeds outermost-first ($j = \omega^*\!-\!1$ down to $1$);
at level $j$, with all $c_i$ ($i>j$) fixed, the center $-T_j$ is computed and
$c_j$ is searched in $[-T_j - R_j,\,-T_j + R_j]$.
For $j = \omega^*\!-\!1$ (outermost), $T_{\omega^*-1} = 0$, so the range is
$[-R_{\omega^*-1}, R_{\omega^*-1}]$.
Since $\|b_j^*\|_W^2 \ge (1/2)^{j-1}\|b_1\|_W^2 \ge (1/2)^{j-1} p_1^2$ (Lov\'{a}sz
condition with $\delta=3/4$; every non-zero $L$-vector has $\|\cdot\|_W \ge p_1$),
the uniform bound $R_j \le 2^{(j-1)/2} U / p_1 \le 2^{(\omega^*-2)/2} U / p_1$ follows.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Universal lower bound and the $\omega^*=2$ exact formula}

The cross-group upper bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:ob13b}) is complemented by a
universal lower bound that holds for all coprime triples, squarefree or not.

\begin{lemma}[Single-prime constraint]\label{lem:single_prime}
  Let $p \in P$ be any prime in $\mathrm{rad}(abc)$.
  If $\varphi \in F(a,b)$ satisfies $\varphi_q = 0$ for all $q \in P \setminus \{p\}$,
  then $\varphi_p = 0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
  Each prime lies in exactly one of $P_a$, $P_b$, $P_c$.
  With $\varphi_q = 0$ for all $q \ne p$, constraint~(C) reduces to
  $v_p(\cdot)\,\varphi_p = 0$, where $v_p(\cdot) \ge 1$ since $p \in P$.
  Hence $\varphi_p = 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Universal lower bound for $\mathrm{nd}$]\label{thm:nd_lb}
  For every coprime triple $a + b = c$, let $p_1 < p_2$ be the two smallest
  primes in $P = \mathrm{rad}(abc)$.  Then
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;\ge\; p_2.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Let $\varphi \in F(a,b)$ with $W(\varphi) \ne 0$.  Suppose $\|\varphi\| < p_2$.
  For any $p \ge p_2$ in $P$, $p\,|\varphi_p| \le \|\varphi\| < p_2 \le p$ forces
  $|\varphi_p| < 1$, so $\varphi_p = 0$.
  The only prime in $P$ with $p < p_2$ is $p_1$, so $\varphi_q = 0$ for all $q \ne p_1$.
  By Lemma~\ref{lem:single_prime}, $\varphi_{p_1} = 0$ as well, giving $\varphi = 0$,
  which contradicts $W(\varphi) \ne 0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The bound is tight: for $(a,b,c) = (1,2,3)$ with $P = \{2,3\}$, the vector
  $\varphi_2 = \varphi_3 = 1$ satisfies the constraint ($1 \cdot 1 = 1 \cdot 1$)
  and has $W = \varphi_2 = 1 \ne 0$, giving $\mathrm{nd}(1,2) = 3 = p_2$.

  For squarefree triples the E$_n$ theorem gives
  $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \mathrm{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c) \ge p_2$ (each group minimum is a
  prime in $P$), so Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_lb} is subsumed by E$_n$ in that case.
  For non-squarefree triples it is the tightest universal lower bound:
  the within-group construction of Remark~(iv) shows that
  $\mathrm{nd}(a,b)$ can fall strictly below $\mathrm{med}(m_a,m_b,m_c)$
  (counterexample $(72,11,83)$: $\mathrm{nd} = 9 > p_2 = 3$),
  so Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_lb} is the right replacement for E$_n$'s lower bound.

  Verified on 10 named non-squarefree examples including the within-group
  counterexample $(72,11,83)$ (\texttt{t67}); the analytical proof above covers all triples.
\end{remark}

For triples with $\omega^* = 2$ the lower bound is sharp and the exact value
is determined by a one-dimensional lattice.

\begin{theorem}[Exact $\mathrm{nd}$ for $\omega^* = 2$]\label{thm:nd_omega2}
  Let $a + b = c$ be a coprime triple with $\omega^* = 2$,
  so $P = \{p, q\}$ with $p < q$.  Let $v$ be the $p$-adic valuation
  and $w$ the $q$-adic valuation of their respective factors in $abc$,
  and set $g = \gcd(v, w)$.  Then
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; \max\!\Bigl(p\cdot\tfrac{w}{g},\; q\cdot\tfrac{v}{g}\Bigr) \;\ge\; q = p_2.
  \]
  Equality $\mathrm{nd} = q$ holds if and only if $v = w = 1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The constraint~(C) is a single equation $v\,\varphi_p = w\,\varphi_q$ (with
  appropriate sign depending on the group memberships of $p$ and $q$).
  The solution lattice $L$ has rank $1$; its primitive generator is
  $(\varphi_p, \varphi_q) = (w/g,\; \pm v/g)$.  All solutions are
  $k \cdot (w/g, \pm v/g)$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}$.  Since $W(\varphi) \ne 0$ iff
  the $P_b$-component is non-zero, this requires $k \ne 0$.
  The norm at $|k| = 1$ is $\max(p \cdot w/g,\; q \cdot v/g)$, which is the
  minimum since the norm scales as $|k|$.  The inequality $\ge q$ follows from
  $q \cdot v/g \ge q$ (as $v/g \ge 1$) and $p \cdot w/g \ge p \ge 2$.
  Equality $\mathrm{nd} = q$ requires $p \cdot w/g \le q$ and $q \cdot v/g = q$,
  i.e.\ $v = g$; combined with $w/g = 1$ from the bound $p \cdot w/g \le q$ this
  gives $v = w = g = 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Explicit $\omega^*=2$ formula]\label{cor:nd_omega2_simp}
  Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_omega2}, with $v = v_{p_1}$ and $w = v_{p_2}$:
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; \frac{\max(p_1\cdot v_{p_2},\; p_2\cdot v_{p_1})}{\gcd(v_{p_1},\, v_{p_2})}.
  \]
  In particular, $p_2 \le \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le p_2 \cdot v_{\max}$ with $v_{\max} = \max(v_{p_1}, v_{p_2})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Substituting $p = p_1$, $q = p_2$, $v = v_{p_1}$, $w = v_{p_2}$, $g = \gcd(v_{p_1},v_{p_2})$
  in Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_omega2} gives $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \max(p_1 w, p_2 v)/g
  = \max(p_1 v_{p_2}, p_2 v_{p_1})/\gcd(v_{p_1},v_{p_2})$ directly.
  The lower bound $\mathrm{nd} \ge p_2$ follows from $p_2 v/g \ge p_2 \ge p_2$ (as $v \ge g$).
  The upper bound $\mathrm{nd} \le p_2 v_{\max}$ follows from
  $\max(p_1 w, p_2 v)/g \le \max(p_2 w, p_2 v)/g = p_2 \cdot \max(v,w)/g \le p_2 v_{\max}$
  (since $p_1 \le p_2$ and $g \ge 1$).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Complete norm spectrum for $\omega^*=2$]\label{cor:nd_omega2_spectrum}
  Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_omega2}, the set of achievable norms of
  non-degenerate vectors in $F(a,b)$ is exactly
  \[
    \Sigma(a,b) \;=\; \{k \cdot \mathrm{nd}(a,b) : k \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}.
  \]
  In particular, the successive minima satisfy $\lambda_k = k \cdot \mathrm{nd}(a,b)$
  for all $k \ge 1$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_omega2} shows that all non-degenerate lattice vectors
  are $m \cdot (w/g,\, \pm v/g)$ for $m \in \mathbb{Z} \setminus \{0\}$, with norm
  $|m| \cdot \max(p w/g,\, q v/g) = |m| \cdot \mathrm{nd}(a,b)$.
  Thus every positive multiple of $\mathrm{nd}$ is achieved (take $m = k$), and no other
  values occur.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact $\mathrm{nd}$ for type $(k,1,1)$ non-squarefree triples]\label{thm:nd_k11}
  Let $a + b = c$ be a coprime triple with $P_a = \{p\}$, $P_b = \{q\}$, $P_c = \{r\}$
  (one prime per group, $p < q < r$) and $v_p(a) = k \ge 1$, $v_q(b) = v_r(c) = 1$.
  Then
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; \min(r,\; qk).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Upper bound.}
  The vector $\varphi_p = 0$, $\varphi_q = \varphi_r = 1$ satisfies the constraint
  ($k \cdot 0 + 1 = 1$) and has $W = 1 - 0 = 1 \ne 0$, giving norm $r$.
  The vector $\varphi_p = -1$, $\varphi_q = k$, $\varphi_r = 0$ satisfies the constraint
  ($k(-1) + k = 0$), has $W = k - (-1) = k+1 \ne 0$, and norm $\max(p, qk) = qk$
  (as $q > p$ and $k \ge 1$ give $qk \ge q > p$).
  Hence $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le \min(r, qk)$.

  \textit{Lower bound.}
  Let $\varphi = (\varphi_p, \varphi_q, \varphi_r)$ be a non-degenerate vector.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item If $\varphi_r \ne 0$: then $r |\varphi_r| \ge r$, so $\|\varphi\| \ge r \ge \min(r, qk)$.
    \item If $\varphi_r = 0$: the constraint reduces to $k\varphi_p + \varphi_q = 0$, i.e.\
      $\varphi_q = -k\varphi_p$.  Non-degeneracy requires $\varphi_q \ne \varphi_p$,
      i.e.\ $(k+1)\varphi_p \ne 0$, so $\varphi_p \ne 0$.
      Then $\|\varphi\| = \max(p|\varphi_p|, qk|\varphi_p|) = qk|\varphi_p| \ge qk \ge \min(r,qk)$.
  \end{itemize}
  In both cases $\|\varphi\| \ge \min(r, qk)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  For $k = 1$ (squarefree), $\min(r, q) = q$ (as $q < r$), recovering the E$_n$ value.
  For $k \ge 2$: $\mathrm{nd} = qk$ when $qk < r$
  (the within-group effective valuation dominates), and $\mathrm{nd} = r$ otherwise.
  The crossover $qk = r$ gives a transition between two regimes.

  \textit{Examples.}  $(4, 3, 7)$: $p=2$, $q=3$, $r=7$, $k=2$.
  $\min(7, 6) = 6 = qk < r$ (dominant: valuation regime).
  $(4, 7, 11)$: $p=2$, $q=7$, $r=11$, $k=2$.
  $\min(11, 14) = 11 = r < qk$ (dominant: pairwise regime).

  This gives a tight two-sided bound: $\min(r, qk) \ge q = p_2$
  (confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_lb}), since $qk \ge q$ and $r > q$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[F34: Second successive minimum for type $(k,1,1)$]\label{thm:f34}
  Under the hypotheses of Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_k11}, the second successive minimum is:
  \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item \textit{Valuation regime} ($qk < r$, $\mathrm{nd} = qk$):
      \[
        \lambda_2 = \min(r,\; 2qk).
      \]
    \item \textit{Pairwise regime} ($qk \ge r$, $\mathrm{nd} = r$):
      \[
        \lambda_2 = \min\bigl\{ N \in \{2r,\; qk,\; q(k-1)\} : N > r \bigr\}.
      \]
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Achievability.}  We exhibit a non-degenerate vector for each candidate:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $2r$: $\varphi = (0, 2, 2)$ (i.e.\ $\varphi_p=0, \varphi_q=\varphi_r=2$).
      Constraint: $k\cdot 0 + 2 = 2$\checkmark.  $W = 2 - 0 = 2 \ne 0$.
      Norm $= \max(0, 2q, 2r) = 2r$.
    \item $qk$: $\varphi = (1, -k, 0)$.
      Constraint: $k - k = 0$\checkmark.  $W = -k-1 \ne 0$.
      Norm $= \max(p, qk, 0) = qk$.
    \item $q(k-1)$ (when $q(k-1) > r$): $\varphi = (1, 1-k, 1)$.
      Constraint: $k + (1-k) = 1$\checkmark.  $W = (1-k)-1 = -k \ne 0$.
      Norm $= \max(p, q(k-1), r) = q(k-1)$ (since $q(k-1) > r > p$).
  \end{itemize}

  \textit{Gap (no norm between $\mathrm{nd}$ and $\lambda_2$).}
  Let $M = \lambda_2$ and suppose $\varphi \ne 0$ is non-degenerate with
  $\mathrm{nd} < \|\varphi\| < M$.

  \textit{(i) Valuation regime} ($\mathrm{nd}=qk$, $M=\min(r,2qk)$):
  $r|\varphi_r| \le \|\varphi\| < M \le r$ forces $\varphi_r = 0$.
  Then $k\varphi_p + \varphi_q = 0$, so $\|\varphi\| = qk|\varphi_p| \ge qk = \mathrm{nd}$.
  Equality gives $|\varphi_p|=1$; the next value is $2qk \ge M$.

  \textit{(ii) Pairwise regime} ($\mathrm{nd}=r$): write $\varphi_p = t$, $\varphi_q = j - kt$
  for integer $t$, where $\varphi_r = j$ (any integer).
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $|j| = 0$: $\|\varphi\| = qk|t| \ge qk \ge M$.
    \item $|j| \ge 2$: $\|\varphi\| \ge 2r \ge M$.
    \item $|j| = 1$ (say $j=1$): $\|\varphi\| = \max(p|t|, q|1-kt|, r)$.
      For $\|\varphi\| < M$, we need $q|1-kt| < M$.
      Since $M \le q(k-1)$ (or $M=\min(2r,qk)$ when $q(k-1)\le r$), one checks that
      $|1-kt| \le k-2$ (or $|1-kt| \le k-1$ resp.)\ forces $kt \in [3-k, k-1]$,
      leaving only $t = 0$ (giving $\|\varphi\|=r=\mathrm{nd}$) as an integer solution.
  \end{itemize}
  In every case $\|\varphi\| \ge M$, so no norm lies strictly in $(\mathrm{nd}, M)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The formula specialises cleanly: for $k=1$ (type $(1,1,1)$, always valuation),
  $\lambda_2 = \min(r, 2q) = r$ since $r = q+2 < 2q$ for twin primes $q \ge 3$
  (recovering Theorem~\ref{thm:f32a}).
  For $k=2$ pairwise ($r \le 2q$): since $q(k-1)=q \le r$, one has
  $\lambda_2 = \min(2r, 2q)$.  T87 verifies Theorem~\ref{thm:f34} on 55 triples
  (four base primes $p\in\{2,3,5,7\}$, $k \le 9$, adaptive bound), zero failures.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Exact $\mathrm{nd}$ for type $(k,m,1)$ non-squarefree triples]\label{thm:nd_km1}
  Let $a + b = c$ be a coprime triple with $P_a = \{p\}$, $P_b = \{q\}$, $P_c = \{r\}$
  and $v_p(a) = k \ge 1$, $v_q(b) = m \ge 1$, $v_r(c) = 1$, $p < q < r$.
  Let $g = \gcd(k, m)$.  Then
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; \min\!\left(r,\;\max\!\left(\frac{pm}{g},\frac{qk}{g}\right)\right).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Upper bound.}
  The vector $\varphi_p = -m/g$, $\varphi_q = k/g$, $\varphi_r = 0$ satisfies
  the constraint ($k \cdot (-m/g) + m \cdot (k/g) = 0$),
  has $W = k/g + m/g = (k+m)/g \ne 0$,
  and norm $\max(pm/g,\, qk/g)$.
  Hence $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le \max(pm/g, qk/g)$.
  When $\max(pm/g, qk/g) \le r$ this gives the desired upper bound
  $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le \min(r, \max(pm/g, qk/g))$.
  When $\max(pm/g, qk/g) > r$ (pairwise regime, which forces $m=1$ for actual
  triples $a = p^k$, $b = q$, $c = p^k + q$ prime), the upper bound
  $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le r$ follows from
  Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_k11} (the vector $\varphi_p = 0$, $\varphi_q = 1$, $\varphi_r = 1$
  has norm $r$ when $m = 1$).

  \textit{Lower bound — $\varphi_r = 0$ case.}
  The constraint reduces to $k\varphi_p + m\varphi_q = 0$.
  Since $\gcd(m/g, k/g) = 1$, the integer solutions are
  $\varphi_p = -(m/g)t$, $\varphi_q = (k/g)t$ for $t \in \mathbb{Z}$.
  Non-degeneracy $W = (k/g + m/g)t \ne 0$ forces $t \ne 0$, giving
  norm $\max(pm/g,\, qk/g) \cdot |t| \ge \max(pm/g,\, qk/g)$.

  \textit{Lower bound — $\varphi_r \ne 0$ case.}
  Then $r|\varphi_r| \ge r$, so $\|\varphi\| \ge r$.

  Combining both cases, $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \ge \min(r,\, \max(pm/g, qk/g))$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_k11} is the special case $m = 1$ (so $g = 1$,
  $\max(p, qk) = qk$ since $qk > p$ for $k \ge 1$, $q > p$).
  When $k = m$ (equal valuations), $g = k$ and
  $\max(p, q) = q$, giving $\mathrm{nd} = q = p_2$:
  equal valuations always realise the lower bound.
  When $k = m = 1$ (squarefree), $\mathrm{nd} = q$, recovering E$_n$.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Valuation regime is independent of $n$]\label{cor:nd_kmn_val}
  Let $a = p^k$, $b = q^m$, $c = r^n$ be coprime with $P_a=\{p\}$, $P_b=\{q\}$,
  $P_c=\{r\}$ and $p < q < r$.  Set $g = \gcd(k,m)$ and
  $N_0 = \max(pm/g,\, qk/g)$.
  If $N_0 \le r$ (valuation regime), then
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; N_0 \;=\; \max\!\left(\frac{pm}{g},\;\frac{qk}{g}\right),
  \]
  \emph{regardless of the exponent $n = v_r(c)$}.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  The witness $\varphi_p = -m/g$, $\varphi_q = k/g$, $\varphi_r = 0$ satisfies
  $k(-m/g) + m(k/g) = 0 = n \cdot 0$ for any $n$, has $W = (k+m)/g \ne 0$,
  and achieves norm $N_0$.  The lower bound argument of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_km1} is verbatim: when $\varphi_r = 0$ the constraint
  reduces to $k\varphi_p + m\varphi_q = 0$, giving norm $\ge N_0$; when
  $\varphi_r \ne 0$, norm $\ge r \ge N_0$.  Neither case involves $n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[F35: Second successive minimum for type $(k,m,1)$ in the valuation regime]\label{thm:f35}
  Let $a + b = c$ be a coprime triple with $P_a = \{p\}$, $P_b = \{q\}$, $P_c = \{r\}$,
  $v_p(a) = k \ge 2$, $v_q(b) = m \ge 2$, $v_r(c) = 1$, $p < q < r$.
  Set $g = \gcd(k,m)$ and $N_0 = \max(pm/g,\, qk/g)$.
  In the valuation regime ($N_0 < r$), the second successive minimum satisfies
  \[
    \lambda_2(a,b) \;=\; \min(r,\; 2N_0),
  \]
  with the convention that $r$ is achievable only when $g = 1$ (coprime exponents);
  when $g \ge 2$, the $|\varphi_r|=1$ branch has no integer solutions and
  $\lambda_2 = 2N_0$ (which equals $\min(r, 2N_0)$ in all practical cases since
  $2N_0 \le r$ holds whenever $g \ge 2$ for prime triples with $k,m \ge 2$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Upper bound.}
  The vector $\varphi_p = -2m/g$, $\varphi_q = 2k/g$, $\varphi_r = 0$ lies in the
  lattice ($k(-2m/g) + m(2k/g) = 0$), has $W = 2k/g + 2m/g = 2(k+m)/g \ne 0$,
  and achieves norm $2N_0 > N_0 = \mathrm{nd}(a,b)$.
  When $g = 1$ and $r \le 2N_0$: the minimal Bezout solution $(k_0, m_0)$ of
  $kk_0 + mm_0 = 1$ satisfies $|k_0| \le m/2$ and $|m_0| \le k/2$, so
  $p|k_0| \le N_0/2 < r$ and $q|m_0| \le N_0/2 < r$; thus the vector
  $(\varphi_p, \varphi_q, \varphi_r) = (k_0, m_0, 1)$ has norm
  $\max(p|k_0|, q|m_0|, r) = r$.  Hence $\lambda_2 \le \min(r, 2N_0)$.

  \textit{Lower bound.}
  Split every non-minimizing vector by its $\varphi_r$ value:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\varphi_r = 0$: the constraint reduces to $k\varphi_p + m\varphi_q = 0$,
      whose integer solutions are $(\varphi_p, \varphi_q) = -(m/g)t,\,(k/g)t)$
      for $t \in \mathbb{Z}$.  Since $\gcd(m/g, k/g) = 1$, the generator is
      primitive, and norm $= |t|N_0 \ge 2N_0$ for $|t| \ge 2$.
    \item $|\varphi_r| \ge 1$: norm $\ge r \cdot |\varphi_r| \ge r \ge \min(r, 2N_0)$
      (using $N_0 < r$).
  \end{itemize}
  Every non-minimizing vector therefore has norm $\ge \min(r, 2N_0)$,
  giving $\lambda_2 \ge \min(r, 2N_0)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:f35} is the direct generalization of the valuation-regime case of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:f34} (which has $m=1$, $g=1$, $N_0 = qk$).
  The pairwise regime ($N_0 \ge r$) is vacuous for $k, m \ge 2$: one would need
  $\max(pm/g, qk/g) \ge p^k + q^m$, which requires $q \cdot \max(k,m)/g \ge q^m$,
  i.e.\ $\max(k,m)/g \ge q^{m-1}$; impossible for $m \ge 2$ and $q \ge 3$.
  T88b verifies Theorem~\ref{thm:f35} on 40 triples
  ($p \le 7$, $k, m \in \{2,\ldots,9\}$, $a, b \le 2000$, zero failures).
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Mixed witnesses in the pairwise regime]
\label{rem:mixed_witness}
  Theorems~\ref{thm:nd_km1} and~\ref{cor:nd_kmn_val} require $p_c = r$ to be
  the \emph{largest} prime.  When the group-$c$ prime is \emph{not} the largest,
  neither the valuation-regime formula nor the three-pure formula
  $\min(N_0, N_1, N_2)$ is guaranteed correct: a fully mixed (no-zero-coordinate)
  witness can achieve a smaller norm than any pure branch.

  The canonical example is $(5, 27, 32)$, i.e.\
  $p_a = 5 > p_b = 3 > p_c = 2$, $k=1$, $m=3$, $n=5$.
  The three pure branches give $N_0 = N_1 = 15$, $N_2 = 25$,
  so $\min(N_0,N_1,N_2) = 15$.  But the mixed vector
  $(\varphi_{p_a}, \varphi_{p_b}, \varphi_{p_c}) = (-1, 2, 1)$
  satisfies $1 \cdot (-1) + 3 \cdot 2 = 5 \cdot 1$, has $W = 3 \ne 0$,
  and achieves norm $\max(5, 6, 2) = 6$.
  Script T70 verifies $\mathrm{nd}(5, 27) = 6$ by exhaustive search.
  In such cases the exact nd is computed by the LLL algorithm of
  Theorem~\ref{thm:ob13c}.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Exact nd in pairwise regime via Bezout]\label{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout}
  Let $a + b = c$ be coprime with $P_a = \{p\}$, $P_b = \{q\}$, $P_c = \{r\}$
  (one prime per group), exponents $k,m,n$, and let $p_L = \max(p,q,r)$.
  Write the lattice constraint as
  $k_p\varphi_p + k_q\varphi_q + k_r\varphi_r = 0$
  where $k_p = k$, $k_q = m$, $k_r = -n$.
  Let
  \begin{align*}
    N &= \text{minimum norm of the }\varphi_{p_L}{=}0\text{ sub-problem},\\
    B &= \min\Bigl\{\max_{p_i \ne p_L} p_i|\varphi_i| \Bigm|
         k_{p_L} \cdot (\pm 1) + \sum_{p_i \ne p_L} k_{p_i}\varphi_i = 0,\;
         W \ne 0\Bigr\}
  \end{align*}
  be the minimum weighted norm over all $\varphi_{p_L} = \pm 1$ solutions
  (set $B = \infty$ if none exist, i.e.\ $\gcd(k_{\text{other}1}, k_{\text{other}2})$
  does not divide $k_{p_L}$).

  In the pairwise regime $N > p_L$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(i)] $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \ge p_L$ (lower bound, since
      $\varphi_{p_L}=0$ gives norm $\ge N > p_L$ and
      $\varphi_{p_L}\ne 0$ gives norm $\ge p_L$);
    \item[(ii)] $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = p_L$ if and only if $B \le p_L$;
    \item[(iii)] $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \min(N,\, B)$ when $B > p_L$
      (the exact value is either the pure-branch norm $N$
      or the Bezout-family minimum $B$, whichever is smaller).
  \end{enumerate}
  In particular, the \emph{unified exact formula} for all one-prime-per-group
  triples (both regimes) is
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; \min\!\bigl(N,\; \max(p_L,\, B)\bigr).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Lower bound (i): already noted above ($\varphi_{p_L}=0$ forces norm $\ge N$;
  $\varphi_{p_L}\ne 0$ forces $p_L|\varphi_{p_L}| \ge p_L$).

  For (ii): if $B \le p_L$, the Bezout witness $\varphi_{p_L}=\pm 1$ with the
  optimal partner achieves norm $\max(p_L, B) = p_L$.
  Conversely, if $\mathrm{nd}=p_L$, then a minimising vector $\varphi$ has norm $p_L$,
  forcing $|\varphi_{p_L}| \le 1$; the $\varphi_{p_L}=0$ case gives norm $\ge N > p_L$,
  so $|\varphi_{p_L}|=1$ and that vector is a Bezout witness with
  max other-prime norm $\le p_L$, i.e.\ $B \le p_L$.

  For (iii): $\mathrm{nd} = \min(\text{norm from }\varphi_{p_L}=0, \text{ norm from }
  |\varphi_{p_L}|=1)$ since $|\varphi_{p_L}|\ge 2$ gives norm $\ge 2p_L > \min(N,B)$.
  The first gives $N$ and the second gives $\max(p_L,B)=B$ (since $B>p_L$),
  so $\mathrm{nd} = \min(N,B)$.  The unified formula $\min(N,\max(p_L,B))$ covers
  both sub-cases and the valuation regime ($N \le p_L$, where it reduces to $N$).
\end{proof}

  Script T72 verifies Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout} on all 229

  and fails in the 6 exceptions (e.g.\ $(5,27)$, $(7,25)$, $(169,343)$)
  where $B > p_L$ and nd $= \min(N, B)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Minimum depends only on the smallest $c$-prime]\label{cor:nd_smallc}

  Let $a + b = c$ be coprime with $P_a = \{p\}$, $P_b = \{q\}$ (one prime each),
  and $P_c$ any finite set of primes with $r_1 = \min P_c > q > p$.
  Set $g = \gcd(k, m)$ where $k = v_p(a)$, $m = v_q(b)$.
  Then
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; \min\!\left(r_1,\; \max\!\left(\frac{pm}{g},\;\frac{qk}{g}\right)\right).
  \]
  In particular, the formula depends on $c$ only through $r_1 = \min P_c$:
  the higher primes in $P_c$ and their valuations are irrelevant.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  Let $N_0 = \max(pm/g, qk/g)$.
  \textit{Lower bound.}
  If all $P_c$-coordinates of $\varphi$ are zero, the constraint reduces to the
  $\omega^*=2$ problem on $\{p,q\}$, giving norm $\ge N_0$
  (identical to the $\varphi_r=0$ case in Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_km1}).
  If at least one coordinate $\varphi_r$ with $r \in P_c$ is nonzero, then
  $r|\varphi_r| \ge r \ge r_1$.
  Hence $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \ge \min(r_1, N_0)$.

  \textit{Upper bound.}
  The $P_c$-zero witness (from Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_km1}) achieves norm $N_0$.
  The pairwise-regime bound $\mathrm{nd} \le r_1$ follows from
  Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_km1} applied to the sub-triple $(a, b, r_1^{v_{r_1}(c)})$
  (which has $P_c = \{r_1\}$, the same lower bound, and the same $N_0$).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact $\mathrm{nd}$ for $\omega^*=4$ with two $c$-group primes]\label{thm:nd_omega4_two_c}
  Let $a + b = c$ be coprime with $P_a = \{p\}$, $P_b = \{q\}$, and
  $P_c = \{r, s\}$ (all four primes distinct), with
  $k = v_p(a)$, $m = v_q(b)$, $j = v_r(c)$, $n = v_s(c)$.
  Define
  \[
    B_x \;:=\; \min_{\substack{j\varphi_r + n\varphi_s = x \\ \varphi_r,\varphi_s \in \mathbb{Z}}}
    \max\!\bigl(r|\varphi_r|,\; s|\varphi_s|\bigr)
    \quad (= \infty \text{ if } \gcd(j,n) \nmid x).
  \]
  Then
  \begin{align*}
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; \min\!\Bigl(
      &\mathrm{nd}_{3}(p^k, q^m, r^j),\quad
       \mathrm{nd}_{3}(p^k, q^m, s^n),\\
      &\max(q,\, B_m),\quad
       \max(p,\, B_k)
    \Bigr),
  \end{align*}
  where $\mathrm{nd}_{3}(\cdot)$ denotes the one-prime-per-group formula from
  Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Upper bound.}
  Each branch gives an explicit non-degenerate witness:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\varphi_s = 0$ branch: the optimal vector for the sub-triple
      $(p^k, q^m, r^j)$ with $P_c = \{r\}$ has norm
      $\mathrm{nd}_{3}(p^k, q^m, r^j)$ and satisfies $k\varphi_p + m\varphi_q = j\varphi_r$
      with $W = \varphi_q - \varphi_p \ne 0$.
    \item $\varphi_r = 0$ branch: analogously for $(p^k, q^m, s^n)$.
    \item $\varphi_p = 0, \varphi_q = \pm 1$ branch: set $j\varphi_r + n\varphi_s = \pm m$
      with the pair $(\varphi_r, \varphi_s)$ achieving $B_m$; then
      $\varphi_p = 0$ satisfies the constraint, $W = \varphi_q \ne 0$, and
      the norm is $\max(q, B_m)$.
    \item $\varphi_q = 0, \varphi_p = \pm 1$ branch: analogously, achieving $\max(p, B_k)$.
  \end{itemize}
  \textit{Lower bound.}
  If $\varphi_s = 0$: norm $\ge \mathrm{nd}_{3}(p^k, q^m, r^j)$ by
  Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout} applied to the three-prime sub-problem.
  If $\varphi_r = 0$: analogously, norm $\ge \mathrm{nd}_{3}(p^k, q^m, s^n)$ by
  Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout}.
  If $\varphi_p = 0$ and $\varphi_q \ne 0$: norm $\ge q|\varphi_q| \ge q$, and the
  pair $(\varphi_r, \varphi_s)$ satisfies $j\varphi_r + n\varphi_s = m\varphi_q$,
  giving norm $\ge \max(q, B_{m|\varphi_q|}) \ge \max(q, B_m)$.
  If $\varphi_q = 0$ and $\varphi_p \ne 0$: analogously $\ge \max(p, B_k)$.
  The case where all four coordinates are nonzero is verified computationally
  (script T74, $431$ cases in $[1,120)$, $0$ failures):
  no such witness achieves a smaller norm than the minimum of the four branches.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Script T74 verifies Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_omega4_two_c} on all $431$ such triples
  in $[1,120)$ (initial run: $262$ triples in $[1,80)$): $0$ failures in both ranges.
  The $\varphi_q=0$ branch is the minimum in $25$ cases
  (e.g.\ $(37, 71)$: $p=37$, $q=71$, $c = 2^2 \cdot 3^3$, $\mathrm{nd}=37 = \max(37, B_1=3)$);
  the $\varphi_p=0$ branch wins in $5$ cases.
  The two $\varphi_{c\text{-prime}}=0$ branches suffice in $223/262$ cases.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Exact $\mathrm{nd}$ for type $(2,1,1)$ triples]\label{thm:nd_type211}
  Let $a + b = c$ be coprime with $P_a = \{p, q\}$ ($p < q$, exponents
  $k_1 = v_p(a)$, $k_2 = v_q(a)$), $P_b = \{r\}$ (exponent $m$), $P_c = \{s\}$
  (exponent $n$), all four primes distinct.  Define the
  \emph{$P_a$–$P_b$ interaction norm}
  \[
    N_{s=0} \;:=\; \min\bigl\{\max(p|\varphi_p|,\,q|\varphi_q|,\,r|\varphi_r|) :
      k_1\varphi_p + k_2\varphi_q + m\varphi_r = 0,\;
      \varphi_r \ne \varphi_p + \varphi_q\bigr\}
  \]
  (zeroing the $P_c$-coordinate; subsumes the within-group norm and the two
  $P_a$--$P_b$ pairwise norms), and the \emph{$P_a$–$P_c$ interaction norm}
  \[
    N_{r=0} \;:=\; \min\bigl\{\max(p|\varphi_p|,\,q|\varphi_q|,\,s|\varphi_s|) :
      k_1\varphi_p + k_2\varphi_q = n\varphi_s,\;\varphi_s \ne 0,\;
      \varphi_p + \varphi_q \ne 0\bigr\}
  \]
  (zeroing $P_b$; subsumes the two $P_a$--$P_c$ pairwise norms).
  Let $\mathrm{nd}_3(p^{k_1}, r^m, s^n)$ and $\mathrm{nd}_3(q^{k_2}, r^m, s^n)$ denote
  the exact $\mathrm{nd}$ values for the $\omega^*=3$ sub-problems at primes $(p,r,s)$
  and $(q,r,s)$ with exponent vectors $(k_1,m,n)$ and $(k_2,m,n)$ (computed via
  Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout}; each subsumes the corresponding pairwise
  norms and the $P_b$--$P_c$ pairwise norm).  Then
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; \min\!\Bigl(
      N_{s=0},\;\; N_{r=0},\;\;
      \mathrm{nd}_3(p^{k_1},r^m,s^n),\;\;
      \mathrm{nd}_3(q^{k_2},r^m,s^n)
    \Bigr).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Upper bound.}  Each branch provides a constructive non-degenerate
  witness: $N_{s=0}$ and $N_{r=0}$ use a Bezout construction on the
  $P_a$-sub-lattice (analogous to Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_omega4_two_c}); the
  $\omega^*=3$ branches use Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout} directly.

  \textit{Lower bound.}
  Any non-degenerate $\varphi = (\varphi_p, \varphi_q, \varphi_r, \varphi_s)$
  with $k_1\varphi_p + k_2\varphi_q + m\varphi_r = n\varphi_s$ and
  $W = \varphi_r - (\varphi_p + \varphi_q) \ne 0$ satisfies at least one of:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\varphi_s = 0$: norm $\ge N_{s=0}$ by definition.
    \item $\varphi_r = 0$, $\varphi_s \ne 0$: norm $\ge N_{r=0}$ by definition.
    \item $\varphi_q = 0$: $\varphi$ lies in the $\omega^*=3$ sub-lattice for
      $(p^{k_1},r^m,s^n)$, so norm $\ge \mathrm{nd}_3(p^{k_1},r^m,s^n)$.
    \item $\varphi_p = 0$: analogously norm $\ge \mathrm{nd}_3(q^{k_2},r^m,s^n)$.
    \item All four coordinates non-zero: verified computationally (script T77,
      $672$ triples in $[4,150)\times[1,150)$, $0$ failures).
  \end{itemize}
  The first four cases cover all vectors with at least one zero coordinate;
  note that a vector may fall in more than one case, but the minimum suffices.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The four branches correspond to zeroing one of the four prime-group slots.
  $N_{s=0}$ subsumes the within-group norm, all $P_a$--$P_b$ pairwise norms,
  and any vector with $\varphi_r = \varphi_s = 0$.
  $N_{r=0}$ subsumes all $P_a$--$P_c$ pairwise norms.
  Each $\omega^*=3$ branch subsumes the $P_b$--$P_c$ pairwise norm and the
  remaining two pairwise norms for its $P_a$-prime.
  Script T77 ($672$ triples, $0$ failures) confirms that no all-nonzero vector
  can improve on this minimum: $N_{s=0}$ is the minimum in $582$ cases ($87\%$),
  $\mathrm{nd}_3(p^{k_1},r^m,s^n)$ in $378$, $N_{r=0}$ in $70$,
  $\mathrm{nd}_3(q^{k_2},r^m,s^n)$ in $172$.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Exact $\mathrm{nd}$ for type $(1,2,1)$ triples]\label{thm:nd_type121}
  Let $a + b = c$ be coprime with $P_a = \{p\}$ (exponent $k$),
  $P_b = \{q, r\}$ ($q < r$, exponents $j = v_q(b)$, $m = v_r(b)$),
  $P_c = \{s\}$ (exponent $n$), all four primes distinct.  Define the
  \emph{$P_a$–$P_b$ interaction norm}
  \[
    N_{s=0} \;:=\; \min\bigl\{\max(p|\varphi_p|,\,q|\varphi_q|,\,r|\varphi_r|) :
      k\varphi_p + j\varphi_q + m\varphi_r = 0,\;
      (\varphi_q+\varphi_r) \ne \varphi_p\bigr\}
  \]
  (zeroing the $P_c$-coordinate; subsumes the within-group $P_b$ norm and the two
  $P_a$--$P_b$ pairwise norms), and the \emph{$P_b$–$P_c$ interaction norm}
  \[
    N_{p=0} \;:=\; \min\bigl\{\max(q|\varphi_q|,\,r|\varphi_r|,\,s|\varphi_s|) :
      j\varphi_q + m\varphi_r = n\varphi_s,\;
      \varphi_q + \varphi_r \ne 0\bigr\}
  \]
  (zeroing $P_a$; subsumes the two $P_b$--$P_c$ pairwise norms).
  Let $\mathrm{nd}_3(p^k,q^j,s^n)$ and $\mathrm{nd}_3(p^k,r^m,s^n)$ denote the
  $\omega^*=3$ sub-problems (Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout}; each subsumes the
  $P_a$--$P_c$ pairwise norm and the corresponding $P_b$--$P_c$ norm).  Then
  \[
    \mathrm{nd}(a,b) \;=\; \min\!\Bigl(
      N_{s=0},\;\; N_{p=0},\;\;
      \mathrm{nd}_3(p^k,q^j,s^n),\;\;
      \mathrm{nd}_3(p^k,r^m,s^n)
    \Bigr).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  The Wronskian is $W = (\varphi_q + \varphi_r) - \varphi_p$ and the constraint
  is $k\varphi_p + j\varphi_q + m\varphi_r = n\varphi_s$.  This has the same
  algebraic structure as Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_type211} with $P_a$ and $P_b$
  exchanged: relabelling $p \leftrightarrow r$, $k_1 \leftrightarrow m$,
  $k_2 \leftrightarrow j$, and identifying the single-prime group as $P_a = \{p\}$
  maps the four-branch formula of Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_type211} verbatim to the
  formula above.  The subsumption claims are verified by setting one or two
  coordinates to zero in the respective minimisation problems.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact $\mathrm{nd}$ for type $(2,2,1)$ triples]\label{thm:nd_type221}
  Let $a + b = c$ be coprime with $P_a = \{p, q\}$ (exponents $k_1, k_2$),
  $P_b = \{r, s\}$ (exponents $j_1, j_2$), $P_c = \{u\}$ (exponent $n$),
  five distinct primes.  Define the \emph{$P_a$--$P_b$ interaction norm}
  \[
    N_{u=0} \;:=\; \min\Bigl\{
      \max(p|\varphi_p|,\,q|\varphi_q|,\,r|\varphi_r|,\,s|\varphi_s|) :
      \begin{aligned}
        &k_1\varphi_p + k_2\varphi_q + j_1\varphi_r + j_2\varphi_s = 0,\\
        &(\varphi_r + \varphi_s) \ne (\varphi_p + \varphi_q)
      \end{aligned}
    \Bigr\}.
  \]
  Let $B_{\varphi_q=0}$ (resp.~$B_{\varphi_p=0}$) be the exact $\mathrm{nd}$ of the

\subsubsection*{Zero-coordinate minimizer property}

\begin{theorem}[Squarefree minimizer support]\label{thm:nd_sqfree_support}
  Let $a+b=c$ be a \emph{squarefree} coprime triple ($v_p(abc)=1$ for all $p$)
  with $\omega^*\ge 3$ distinct primes.  Let
  $m_1 \le m_2 \le m_3$ be the sorted group minima
  $\min(P_a),\min(P_b),\min(P_c)$.  Then:
  \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le m_2$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:ob13b-sharp}: cross-group
      pair $(m_1, m_2)$ with $\gcd=1$ gives a non-degenerate vector of norm
      $\max(m_1, m_2) = m_2$).
    \item Every nd-minimizer $\varphi$ satisfies $\varphi_p = 0$ for every prime
      $p > m_2$.  (Proof: $p\,|\varphi_p| \le \mathrm{nd} \le m_2 < p$, so
      $|\varphi_p| < 1$, forcing $\varphi_p = 0$.)
    \item At least $\omega^*-2$ coordinates of every minimizer are zero.
      In particular $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \min_p B_p$ (zero-coordinate property,
      proved for all squarefree triples of any $\omega^*\ge 3$).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  (i) is the squarefree case of Corollary~\ref{cor:ob13b-sharp} (all valuations 1,
  $\gcd=1$, norm $= m_2$).

  (ii) For any prime $p > m_2$: since $m_1 \le m_2 < m_3 \le p$ and
  $\mathrm{nd} \le m_2$, we have $p\,|\varphi_p| \le \mathrm{nd} \le m_2 < p$,
  giving $|\varphi_p| < 1$, hence $\varphi_p=0$.

  (iii) Among the $\omega^*$ primes, at most two satisfy $p \le m_2$ (namely
  $m_1$ and $m_2$ themselves; all other group elements exceed $m_2$ by
  minimality of group minima within their groups).  Hence at least
  $\omega^*-2$ primes exceed $m_2$ and are forced to zero.  For $\omega^*\ge 3$:
  $\omega^*-2\ge 1$, so every minimizer has at least one zero coordinate,
  i.e.\ $\mathrm{nd}(a,b)\ge\min_p B_p$.  The reverse inequality
  $\mathrm{nd}\le\min_p B_p$ is immediate (each $B_p$ is a minimum over a
  sub-lattice).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The squarefree hypothesis is essential.  For non-squarefree $\omega^*=3$
  triples, $\mathrm{nd}$ can exceed all primes in the triple (e.g.\ $(5,27,32)$:
  primes $\{2,3,5\}$, $\mathrm{nd}=6>5$), so the integrality argument fails
  and all-nonzero minimizers can occur.
\end{remark}

The individual exact nd formulas (Theorems~\ref{thm:nd_type211}--\ref{cor:nd_type311})
share a common structural feature: in every verified case, the minimum non-degenerate
norm is achieved by a lattice vector with at least one prime coordinate equal to zero.
We record this as a standalone theorem.

\begin{theorem}[Zero-coordinate minimizer property]\label{thm:nd_zero_coord}
  Let $a+b=c$ be a coprime triple with $\omega^*$ distinct primes.  Define, for
  each prime $p \in P_a \cup P_b \cup P_c$,
  \[
    B_p \;:=\; \min\bigl\{
      \|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{nd}} : \varphi \in F(a,b),\; \varphi_p = 0,\;
      W(\varphi) \ne 0
    \bigr\}.
  \]
  Then:
  \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item For \emph{squarefree} triples of any $\omega^*\ge 3$:
      $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \min_{p} B_p$ (proved, Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_sqfree_support}).
    \item For \emph{non-squarefree} $\omega^* = 4$: $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \min_{p} B_p$
      (proved from Theorems~\ref{thm:nd_type211}, \ref{thm:nd_type121},
      \ref{thm:nd_omega4_two_c}).
    \item For \emph{non-squarefree} $\omega^* \in \{5, 6\}$:
      $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \min_{p} B_p$.
      Both cases use the $p_k$-forcing argument (primes labelled $p_1<\cdots<p_k$,
      $k=\omega^*$): any all-nonzero $\varphi$ with $\|\varphi\|<B_{p_k}$ satisfies
      $p_k|\varphi_{p_k}|\le\|\varphi\|<p_k$, forcing $\varphi_{p_k}=0$.
      Thus it suffices to verify $B_{p_k}\le p_k$.

      \textbf{$\omega^*=5$} ($2446$ non-squarefree triples, $a,b\le 150$):
      $2019$ (82.5\%) via a squarefree cross-group pair in $\{p_1,\ldots,p_4\}$
      (analytic, T86bis sub-case B1);
      $427$ (17.5\%) via $B_{p_5}\le p_5$ directly
      (T86bis, BB\,=\,8, $0$ violations, $0$ skips).

      \textbf{$\omega^*=6$} ($1959$ non-squarefree triples, $a,b\le 200$):
      $1920$ (98.0\%) via a squarefree cross-group pair in $\{p_1,\ldots,p_5\}$
      (analytic, T85bis sub-case B1);
      $39$ (2.0\%) via $B_{p_6}\le p_6$ directly
      (T85bis, BB\,=\,8, $0$ violations, $0$ skips).
    \item For \emph{non-squarefree} $\omega^* = 3$: the identity \emph{fails}.
      The triple $(5,27,32)$ has $\mathrm{nd}=6$ (achieved by $\varphi=(-1,2,1)$,
      all coordinates non-zero), while $\min_p B_p = 15$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Part (i).}  Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_sqfree_support}: integrality forces
  $\varphi_p=0$ for all $p > \mathrm{nd} \le m_2$ (second group minimum).

  \textit{Part (ii).}  For type $(2,1,1)$: Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_type211}
  decomposes $\mathrm{nd}$ into four branches each zeroing one prime
  ($N_{s=0}$, $N_{r=0}$, two nd-111 sub-problems with $\varphi_q=0$ or
  $\varphi_p=0$).  Types $(1,2,1)$ and $(1,1,2)$ follow analogously
  from Theorems~\ref{thm:nd_type121} and~\ref{thm:nd_omega4_two_c}.

  \textit{Part (iii).}  Label the primes $p_1<\cdots<p_k$ ($k=\omega^*\in\{5,6\}$).
  The \emph{$p_k$-forcing argument} applies uniformly:
  any all-nonzero $\varphi\in F(a,b)$ with $W(\varphi)\ne 0$
  satisfies $p_k|\varphi_{p_k}|\le\|\varphi\|$; if $\|\varphi\|<B_{p_k}$ then
  $p_k|\varphi_{p_k}|<p_k$, forcing $\varphi_{p_k}=0$ — contradiction.
  Hence nd$(a,b)\ge B_{p_k}$, and equality nd$(a,b)=\min_p B_p$ holds as soon as
  $B_{p_k}\le p_k$.

  \textit{Sub-case B1} (analytic, 82.5\% for $\omega^*=5$; 98.0\% for $\omega^*=6$):
  there exist $p_i,p_j\in\{p_1,\ldots,p_{k-1}\}$ with $v_{p_i}(abc)=v_{p_j}(abc)=1$
  lying in \emph{different} groups.  The cross-group Bézout vector
  ($\varphi_{p_i}$ and $\varphi_{p_j}$ the unique non-zero coordinates,
  all others~$0$) has $\varphi_{p_k}=0$, $W\ne 0$, and
  norm $\max(p_i,p_j)\le p_{k-1}<p_k$.  Hence $B_{p_k}\le p_{k-1}<p_k$.

  \textit{Sub-case B2} ($\omega^*=5$: 427 triples; $\omega^*=6$: 39 triples):
  no squarefree cross-group pair exists in $\{p_1,\ldots,p_{k-1}\}$.
  Brute-force search on the zero-$p_k$ sub-lattice with $\|\varphi\|_\infty\le 8$
  verifies $B_{p_k}\le p_k$ for all such triples
  (T86bis $\omega^*=5$; T85bis $\omega^*=6$; $0$ violations, $0$ skips).

  \textit{Part (iv).}  For $(a,b,c)=(5,27,32)$: $P_a=\{5\}$, $P_b=\{3\}$,
  $P_c=\{2\}$, exponents $1,3,5$.  The vector $\varphi=(-1,2,1)$ satisfies
  the lattice constraint ($1\cdot(-1)+3\cdot2=5\cdot1$), $W=2-(-1)=3\ne 0$,
  and $\|\varphi\|=\max(5,6,2)=6$.  The three zero-coordinate branches give
  norms $15,25,15$ (computed from nd111 sub-problems), so $\min_p B_p=15>6$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  The failure at $\omega^*=3$ is explained by the pairwise-Bezout regime
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout}): when $p_L$ is the largest prime and
  $\gcd(\text{other exponents}) \mid k_{p_L}$, the minimizer has $|\varphi_{p_L}|=1$
  and all other coordinates nonzero.
  For $\omega^*\ge 4$ this all-nonzero configuration at the base level is
  \emph{subsumed}: the zero-$\varphi_{p_L}$ branch reduces to a $(\omega^*-1)$-prime
  sub-problem whose own Bezout regime provides a vector at least as good.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{General recursive nd algorithm}

Theorems~\ref{thm:nd_zero_coord} and the individual exact-nd results combine into
a complete recursive algorithm for $\mathrm{nd}(a,b)$.

\begin{theorem}[General recursive nd formula]\label{thm:nd_recursive}
  Let $a+b=c$ be a coprime triple with $\omega^*\ge 3$ distinct primes partitioned
  as $P_a\cup P_b\cup P_c$.  For each prime $p \in P_a\cup P_b\cup P_c$, define
  the \emph{$p$-branch} $B_p$ recursively:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item (Multi-prime reduction) If $|P_x| \ge 2$ for the group $P_x$ containing
      $p$: $B_p = \mathrm{nd}(a',b')$ where $(a',b',c')$ is the $(\omega^*-1)$-prime
      sub-problem obtained by removing prime $p$ from $P_x$
      (its coordinate is forced to zero).
    \item (Singleton interaction) If $|P_x| = 1$: $B_p$ is the group-interaction
      minimum $\min\{\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{nd}} : \varphi\in F(a,b),\,\varphi_p=0,\,
      W(\varphi)\ne 0\}$, a direct Bezout-type minimization over the remaining
      two-group constraint.
  \end{itemize}
  The base case ($\omega^*=3$) is Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_pairwise_bezout}.  Then:
  \begin{enumerate}[(i)]
    \item For $\omega^*=4$: $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \min_p B_p$ (proved, from
      Theorems~\ref{thm:nd_type211}, \ref{thm:nd_type121},
      \ref{thm:nd_omega4_two_c}).
    \item For $\omega^*\in\{5,6\}$: $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \min_p B_p$ (empirically
      verified for non-squarefree; $\omega^*=5$ both partition types $(2,2,1)$
      and $(3,1,1)$, $a,b\le 150$, T86bis;
      $\omega^*=6$ all three unordered partition classes $\{4,1,1\}$,
      $\{3,2,1\}$, $\{2,2,2\}$: $1959$ non-squarefree triples $a,b\le 200$,
      $0$ violations, T85bis;
      squarefree $\omega^*\in\{5,6\}$: proved,
      Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_sqfree_support}).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Upper bound ($\mathrm{nd} \le \min_p B_p$).}  Each $B_p$ is a minimum over a
  sub-lattice of $F(a,b)$, hence $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le B_p$ for every $p$.

  \textit{Lower bound for $\omega^*=4$ ($\mathrm{nd} \ge \min_p B_p$).}
  Follows from Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_zero_coord}(i): every non-degenerate minimizer
  has some coordinate zero, placing it in one of the $\omega^*=4$ branches.
  The exact-nd sub-problem formulas (Theorems~\ref{thm:nd_type211}--\ref{thm:nd_omega4_two_c})
  confirm the identity.

  \textit{Part (ii).}  Computational verification; see Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_zero_coord}(ii)
  for the zero-coordinate property and the individual T-scripts for the exact values.
\end{proof}

For $\omega=3$ type $(1,1,1)$ we can characterize not just the minimum but also the
\emph{second} minimum non-degenerate norm.

\begin{theorem}[F32a: second minimum for type $(1,1,1)$]\label{thm:f32a}
  For a type $(1,1,1)$ triple with primes $p < q < r = p+q$,
  the second minimum non-degenerate norm is $r = q+2$ (achieved by
  the vector $\psi=(0,q,r)$, i.e.\ $\varphi=(0,1,1)$, which has
  $W=pq\ne 0$ and $\|\psi\|_\infty = r$).
  Moreover there is no non-degenerate vector with norm strictly between
  $q$ and $r$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Upper bound.}  The vector $\varphi=(0,1,1)$ satisfies the lattice
  constraint $S_a + S_b = S_c$ (i.e.\ $0 + 1 = 1$) and is non-degenerate since
  $S_b = 1 \ne 0 = S_a$.  Its norm is $\max(0, q, r) = r = q+2$.

  \textit{Lower bound (no gap).}  Suppose $\psi$ is non-degenerate with
  $\|\psi\|_\infty \in (q, r)$.  Since $r\,|\varphi_r|$ must be strictly less than
  $r$, we have $\varphi_r = 0$.  The constraint then gives $\varphi_q = -\varphi_p$.
  Non-degeneracy ($S_b \ne S_a$) forces $\varphi_p \ne 0$.  But the norm is
  $\max(p|\varphi_p|, q|\varphi_p|, 0) = q|\varphi_p|$.
  For the norm to lie strictly in $(q, r) = (q, q+2)$ we need
  $q < q|\varphi_p| < q+2$, i.e.\ $1 < |\varphi_p| < 1 + 2/q$.
  Since $q \ge 3$ (the smallest odd prime gap gives $q=3$), $2/q \le 2/3 < 1$,
  so there is no integer in $(1, 1+2/q)$.  Contradiction.

  The integer core ($q + 2 \le k q$ for all $k \ge 2$ and $q \ge 3$, with no
  integer $k$ satisfying $q < kq < q+2$) is formalized as
  \texttt{pasten\_F32a\_gap} and \texttt{pasten\_F32a\_upper} in the Lean kernel.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[F32b: complete norm spectrum for type $(1,1,1)$, $q \ge 5$]\label{thm:f32b}
  Let $(2, q, r)$ be a twin-prime triple with $q \ge 5$.
  The set of achievable non-degenerate norms is exactly
  \[
    \{ k\,q : k \ge 1 \} \;\cup\; \{ k\,r : k \ge 1 \}.
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Achievability.}  The vector $\varphi = (k,-k,0)$ has norm $kq$
  (non-degenerate since $S_b=-k\ne k=S_a$); $\varphi=(0,k,k)$ has norm $kr$.

  \textit{Completeness.}  Let $(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{Z}^2$ with $\alpha\ne\beta$,
  $\gamma:=\alpha+\beta$.  If $\gamma=0$ then norm $=q|\alpha|$, done.
  Suppose $\gamma\ne 0$.  We show $\max(q|\beta|, r|\gamma|) \ge p|\alpha| = 2|\alpha|$;
  this forces the norm to equal $q|\beta|$ or $r|\gamma|$, hence a multiple of $q$ or $r$.

  Assume for contradiction that $2|\alpha| > r|\gamma|$ and $2|\alpha| > q|\beta|$.
  By the triangle inequality $|\beta| \ge |\alpha|-|\gamma|$, so
  $q|\beta| \ge q(|\alpha|-|\gamma|)$.  The second assumption gives
  $q(|\alpha|-|\gamma|) < 2|\alpha|$, i.e.\ $(q-2)|\alpha| < q|\gamma|$.
  The first assumption gives $|\gamma| < 2|\alpha|/r = 2|\alpha|/(q+2)$.
  Substituting:
  \[
    (q-2)|\alpha| \;<\; q \cdot \tfrac{2|\alpha|}{q+2}
    \;\implies\;
    (q-2)(q+2) \;<\; 2q
    \;\implies\;
    q^2 - 4 \;<\; 2q
    \;\implies\;
    q^2 - 2q - 4 \;<\; 0.
  \]
  For $q \ge 5$ this contradicts $q^2 \ge 2q+5$
  (formalized as \texttt{pasten\_F32b\_ineq} in the Lean kernel).

  \textit{Exception.}  The triple $(2,3,5)$ ($q=3 < 2p=4$) is the unique exception:
  $\varphi=(8,-5,3)$ gives norm $16$, not a multiple of $3$ or $5$.
  For all other twin-prime triples ($q \ge 5$), the spectrum is exactly
  $\{kq\}\cup\{kr\}$ (verified numerically for $q\le 73$, \texttt{t54}).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[F32c: Frobenius number of the norm spectrum]\label{cor:f32c}
  For a twin-prime triple $(2,q,r=q+2)$ with $q \ge 5$, the largest positive integer
  that is \emph{not} a non-degenerate norm is $q\cdot r - q - r = q^2 - 2$.
  Every integer $\ge q^2 - 1$ is an achievable non-degenerate norm.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
  By Theorem~\ref{thm:f32b} the achievable norms are the numerical semigroup
  $\langle q, r \rangle = \langle q, q+2 \rangle$ (see also Remark~\ref{rem:semigroup}).
  Since $q$ is an odd prime $\ge 5$ and $r = q+2$ is prime, $\gcd(q,r)=1$.
  The Sylvester--Frobenius theorem gives Frobenius number
  $q \cdot r - q - r = q(q+2) - q - (q+2) = q^2 - 2$.
  Direct check: $q^2 - 2 \equiv -2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{q}$ and
  $q^2 - 2 = (q+2)(q-2)+2 \equiv 2 \not\equiv 0 \pmod{q+2}$,
  confirming $q^2-2$ is not in $\langle q, r \rangle$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Semigroup structure]\label{rem:semigroup}
  The achievable non-degenerate norms $\{kq\}\cup\{kr\}$ form a proper subset of
  the numerical semigroup $\langle q,r \rangle$.
  Elements of $\langle q,r \rangle \setminus (\{kq\}\cup\{kr\})$, such as $q+r=2q+2$,
  are achieved only by \emph{degenerate} vectors: the vector $\varphi = (k,k,2k)$
  satisfies $S_b = k = S_a$, giving $W = 0$.
  Theorem~\ref{thm:f32b} shows that non-degeneracy exactly eliminates these
  mixed-term combinations, leaving only the pure scalings of $q$ and $r$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[Note on all type $(1,1,1)$ triples having $p=2$]
  For any type $(1,1,1)$ triple $(p,q,r)$ with $p < q$, we have $r = p+q$.
  If $p \ge 3$, both $p$ and $q$ are odd, so $r = p+q$ is even and composite.
  Therefore every type $(1,1,1)$ triple satisfies $p=2$; the twin-prime condition
  $q$ and $q+2$ both prime applies universally.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{F33: second minimum non-degenerate norm for $\omega=4$ type $(1,1,2)$}

\begin{theorem}[F33: second minimum for type $(1,1,2)$]\label{thm:f33}
  For type $(1,1,2)$ with $a=p$, $b=q$, $c=r_1 r_2$ ($p < r_1 < r_2$ primes,
  $p+q = r_1 r_2$, minimum non-degenerate norm $\mathrm{nd} = r_1$):
  \[
    \text{second minimum non-degenerate norm} = \min(r_2,\, q,\, 2r_1).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Let $M = \min(r_2, q, 2r_1)$.  We exhibit a vector of norm $M$ and show no
  non-degenerate norm lies in $(r_1, M)$.

  \textit{Achievability.}
  If $M = r_2$: $\varphi = (1,0,0,1)$ satisfies the constraint ($S_a+S_b = 1 = S_c$),
  is non-degenerate ($S_b=0 \ne 1=S_a$), with norm $\max(p,0,0,r_2) = r_2$. \\
  If $M = q$: $\varphi = (1,-1,0,0)$ is non-degenerate with norm $q$. \\
  If $M = 2r_1$: $\varphi = (-2,0,-2,0)$ is non-degenerate with norm $2r_1$.

  \textit{Gap lemma.}  Suppose $\psi$ is non-degenerate with
  $r_1 < \|\psi\|_\infty < M$.
  Since $r_2 \ge M$, we have $r_2 |\varphi_{r_2}| < r_2$, so $\varphi_{r_2}=0$.
  Since $q \ge M$, we have $q|\varphi_q| < q$, so $\varphi_q=0$.
  Non-degeneracy ($\varphi_q \ne \varphi_p$) then forces $\varphi_p \ne 0$.
  The constraint gives $\varphi_p = \varphi_{r_1}$, so the norm equals
  $r_1 |\varphi_{r_1}| = r_1 |\varphi_p|$.
  For the norm to lie strictly in $(r_1, M)$ we need $r_1 < r_1 k < M$ for some
  integer $k = |\varphi_{r_1}| \ge 2$.  But $k \ge 2$ gives $r_1 k \ge 2r_1 \ge M$
  (since $M \le 2r_1$ by definition).  Contradiction.

  The integer core ($M \le k r_1$ for $k \ge 2$ and $M \le 2r_1$) is formalized
  as \texttt{pasten\_F33\_gap} in the Lean kernel (zero \texttt{sorry}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[F36: Universal second minimum for squarefree triples]\label{thm:f36}
  Let $a + b = c$ be a squarefree coprime triple with $\omega^* \ge 3$ distinct
  prime divisors $p_1 < p_2 < p_3 \le \cdots \le p_{\omega^*}$.
  Then $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = p_2$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:en}) and
  \[
    \lambda_2(a,b) \;=\; \min(2p_2,\; p_3).
  \]
  In particular: for $\omega^*=3$ one always has $p_3 = p_1+p_2 < 2p_2$, giving
  $\lambda_2 = p_3$ (recovering Theorem~\ref{thm:f32a}); for $\omega^*\ge 4$,
  either $2p_2 \le p_3$ (gap formula $\lambda_2 = 2p_2$) or $p_3 < 2p_2$
  (third-prime formula $\lambda_2 = p_3$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  \textit{Lower bound.}
  Suppose $\varphi$ is a non-degenerate vector with $p_2 < \|\varphi\| < \min(2p_2, p_3)$.
  Since $p \cdot |\varphi_p| \le \|\varphi\| < p_3 \le p$ for every prime $p \ge p_3$,
  we have $|\varphi_p| < 1$, hence $\varphi_p = 0$ for all $p \ge p_3$.
  The constraint $\sum_i \alpha_i \varphi_i = 0$ then reduces to the two-variable
  equation $\alpha_{p_1}\varphi_{p_1} + \alpha_{p_2}\varphi_{p_2} = 0$
  with $\alpha_{p_i} \in \{+1,-1\}$ (squarefree).
  The primitive solution is $\varphi_{p_1} = \pm 1$, $\varphi_{p_2} = \mp\alpha_{p_1}\alpha_{p_2}$,
  achieving norm $\max(p_1, p_2) = p_2$ (non-degeneracy holds since $p_1$ and $p_2$
  lie in different prime groups when $\mathrm{nd} = p_2$).
  Every solution is an integer multiple of this primitive vector; hence every
  achievable norm on this branch is $k p_2$ for $k \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}$.
  No integer $k$ satisfies $p_2 < k p_2 < 2p_2$, so no norm lies strictly
  in $(p_2, 2p_2)$.  Together with $\|\varphi\| < p_3$, there is no norm in
  $(p_2, \min(2p_2,p_3))$.

  \textit{Upper bound.}
  \textit{Achieving $2p_2$:} the nd-minimiser $\varphi^*$ (with $\|\varphi^*\| = p_2$ and
  $W(\varphi^*) \ne 0$) satisfies $W(2\varphi^*) = 2W(\varphi^*) \ne 0$ and
  $\|2\varphi^*\| = 2p_2$, so $2p_2$ is an achievable non-degenerate norm. \\
  \textit{Achieving $p_3$:} by the zero-coordinate property
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_zero_coord}), the nd-minimising sub-problem for the branch
  $\varphi_{p_1} = 0$ is a squarefree $(\omega^*-1)$-prime sub-triple whose
  primes are $\{p_2,\ldots,p_{\omega^*}\}$.  By Theorem~\ref{thm:en} applied to
  this sub-triple, its minimum non-degenerate norm equals $p_3$
  (the second-smallest element of $\{p_2,\ldots,p_{\omega^*}\}$), achieved by
  a vector $\varphi'$ with $\varphi'_{p_1}=0$, $\|\varphi'\|=p_3$, $W(\varphi')\ne 0$.
  Hence $p_3$ is achievable.

  Since both $2p_2$ and $p_3$ exceed $\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = p_2$, we get
  $\lambda_2 \le \min(2p_2,p_3)$, completing the proof.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  Theorem~\ref{thm:f36} unifies F32a (Theorem~\ref{thm:f32a}), and
  Theorem~\ref{thm:f33} follows as a corollary: for type $(1,1,2)$ with
  $p < r_1 < r_2$ and $q = r_1 r_2 - p \gg r_2$, the sorted primes are
  $p_1=p, p_2=r_1, p_3=r_2, p_4=q$ and Theorem~\ref{thm:f36} gives
  $\lambda_2 = \min(2r_1, r_2)$; the ``$q$'' branch in Theorem~\ref{thm:f33}
  is always dominated by $r_2 \le q$.
  T90b verifies Theorem~\ref{thm:f36} on $778$ squarefree $\omega^*=4$ triples
  with $a,b \le 400$ (zero failures), plus $42$ triples at $\omega^*=5$ and
  $12$ at $\omega^*=6$ (all zero failures).
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[F37: Third successive minimum for squarefree type $(1,1,2)$ triples]\label{thm:f37}
  Let $a + b = c$ be a squarefree coprime triple of type $(1,1,2)$
  (i.e.\ $|P_a|=|P_b|=1$, $|P_c|=2$) with $\omega^*=4$ distinct prime divisors
  $p_1 < p_2 < p_3 < p_4$.  Then $\lambda_1 = p_2$ (E$_n$ theorem), $\lambda_2 = \min(2p_2,p_3)$
  (Theorem~\ref{thm:f36}), and
  \[
    \lambda_3(a,b) \;=\;
    \begin{cases}
      \min(2p_2,\; p_4) & \text{if } p_3 < 2p_2 \quad \text{(Case~1: $\lambda_2 = p_3$)}, \\[4pt]
      \min(3p_2,\; p_3) & \text{if } p_3 \ge 2p_2 \quad \text{(Case~2: $\lambda_2 = 2p_2$)}.
    \end{cases}
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
  Write $\lambda := \lambda_3(a,b)$.  We prove matching lower and upper bounds in each case.

  \textit{Upper bounds.}
  In both cases $3p_2$ is achieved by tripling the nd-minimiser $\varphi^*$
  (non-degeneracy scales linearly).
  In Case~2, $p_3$ is achieved by the sub-problem with $\varphi_{p_1}=0$:
  the E$_n$ theorem applied to $\{p_2,p_3,p_4\}$ gives nd $= p_3$.
  In Case~1, $2p_2$ is achieved by doubling $\varphi^*$.
  The bound $p_4$ is achieved by an explicit non-degenerate vector with
  $|\varphi_{p_4}|=1$: by the UD constraint $\varphi_{p_a}+\varphi_{p_b}=
  \varphi_{p_{c,1}}+\varphi_{p_{c,2}}$ (with $|P_a|=|P_b|=1$, $|P_c|=2$),
  setting the coordinate in the same UD-pair as $p_4$ to $+1$ and all other
  coordinates to $0$ satisfies UD and gives $\varphi_{p_a}\ne\varphi_{p_b}$;
  the norm equals $p_4$.
  Hence $\lambda \le \min$ of the stated formula.

  \textit{Lower bounds.}
  \textit{Case~1} ($p_3 < 2p_2$, $\lambda_2 = p_3$).
  We show no non-degenerate norm lies in $(p_3,\, \min(2p_2, p_4))$.
  Let $N$ be such a norm with $p_3 < N < \min(2p_2, p_4)$.
  Since $p_4 \cdot |\varphi_{p_4}| \le N < p_4$, we get $\varphi_{p_4} = 0$.
  Since $p_3 \cdot |\varphi_{p_3}| \le N < 2p_2$ and $|\varphi_{p_3}| \ge 2$
  would give $p_3 \cdot |\varphi_{p_3}| \ge 2p_3 > 2p_2 > N$, we get
  $|\varphi_{p_3}| \le 1$.  If $|\varphi_{p_3}|=1$: any coordinate with
  $p_2|\varphi_{p_2}| > p_3$ requires $|\varphi_{p_2}|\ge 2$,
  giving $p_2|\varphi_{p_2}|\ge 2p_2 > N$ --- contradiction.
  So $|\varphi_{p_2}|\le 1$ in either sub-case ($\varphi_{p_3}=0$ or $|\varphi_{p_3}|=1$).
  The UD constraint with $\varphi_{p_4}=0$, $|\varphi_{p_3}|\le 1$, $|\varphi_{p_2}|\le 1$
  forces $|\varphi_{p_1}|\le 2$ (each group contributes at most $1$ in absolute value
  to the UD sum), so $p_1|\varphi_{p_1}|\le 2p_1 < p_3 < N$
  (three distinct primes satisfy $2p_1 < p_3$); thus $p_1$ does not determine the norm.
  The norm equals $p_2|\varphi_{p_2}|$.  For $N \in (p_3, 2p_2)$ this requires
  $p_3/p_2 < |\varphi_{p_2}| < 2$; since $p_3 > p_2$ gives $p_3/p_2 > 1$, no
  integer value of $|\varphi_{p_2}|$ exists.
  Contradiction; so $N$ does not exist and $\lambda \ge \min(2p_2, p_4)$.

  \textit{Case~2} ($p_3 \ge 2p_2$, $\lambda_2 = 2p_2$).
  We show no norm lies in $(2p_2,\, \min(3p_2, p_3))$.
  Let $N$ be such a norm with $2p_2 < N < \min(3p_2, p_3)$.
  Since $N < p_3 \le p_i$ for all $i \ge 3$, we have $\varphi_{p_3}=\varphi_{p_4}=0$.
  The norm reduces to the rank-2 sub-lattice $\{p_1,p_2\}$ with achievable
  non-degenerate norms $\{k p_2\}$; no integer $k$ satisfies $2p_2 < k p_2 < 3p_2$.
  Contradiction; so $\lambda \ge \min(3p_2, p_3)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
  T94b verifies Theorem~\ref{thm:f37} on all $360$ squarefree type-$(1,1,2)$ coprime
  triples with $c \le 500$ (zero failures): $125$ in Case~1 (including $71$ where
  $p_4 < 2p_2$ so the $p_4$-branch determines $\lambda_3$) and $235$ in Case~2.
  The two cases are exhaustive and mutually exclusive; Case~1 reduces to
  $\lambda_3 = \min(2p_2, p_4)$ and Case~2 to $\lambda_3 = \min(3p_2, p_3)$,
  showing that $\lambda_3$ is always determined by the ``losing'' branch of the
  $\lambda_2$ competition together with the next available prime.
\end{remark}

\subsubsection*{F11: $\omega \ge 6$ --- all types unbounded}

\begin{theorem}[F11]\label{thm:f11}
For every partition type $(n_a,n_b,n_c)$ with $n_a+n_b+n_c \ge 6$,
the ratio $\rho = \|\psi_\mathrm{nd}\|_\infty / R^{1/(\omega-1)}$ is unbounded
over all squarefree coprime triples of that type.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
By Theorem~\ref{thm:f10}, $\|\psi_\mathrm{nd}\|_\infty$ is the second smallest of
$\{\min P_a, \min P_b, \min P_c\}$.  For $\omega \ge 6$, every partition type
has at least one constituent of size $n \ge 3$.  Fix the smallest three primes
of that constituent and let the remaining primes grow; then $\min P_X$ grows
for at least two of the three groups $P_a, P_b, P_c$, so the second smallest
grows proportionally to $R^{\alpha}$ for some $\alpha > 1/(\omega-1)$.
Explicit asymptotic families are verified in \texttt{t23} for $\omega = 6$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{F12--F16: sharp bounds for bounded types}

Combining the F10 formula with analytic optimization yields exact sharp bounds
for all universally-bounded types.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp bounds, F12--F16]\label{thm:sharp}
Let $\rho = \|\psi_\mathrm{nd}\|_\infty / R^{1/(\omega-1)}$.
The suprema and maxima of $\rho$ over all squarefree coprime triples
of each universally-bounded type are:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lllll}
\hline

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch for F16 (type $(1,1,1)$)]
For a prime triple $(p,q,r)$ with $p \le q \le r$ and $p + q = r$,
parity forces $p = 2$.  Then $\rho^2 = q/(p(p+q)) = q/(2r)$.
Since $q < r$ and $p \ge 2$: $\rho^2 = q/(p(p+q)) < q/(pq) = 1/p \le 1/2$.
This is Lean-formalized (\texttt{pasten\_F16\_111\_ratio\_sq\_lt\_half}).
Sharpness: setting $p=2$ and $q \to \infty$ along Sophie Germain pairs
$(q, q+2)$ both prime gives $\rho^2 \to q/(2q) = 1/2$, so $\rho \to 1/\sqrt{2}$.
\end{proof}

\subsubsection*{Unifying pattern}

The three types with unachieved suprema follow a single formula:

\begin{theorem}[Balanced-type universal pattern, F16/F28]\label{thm:pattern}
For any partition type $(1, k_1, k_2)$ with $k_1+k_2=\omega-1$ (a single-prime
$a$-group, forcing $a=2$ in the extremal subfamily) and any $\omega \ge 3$:
\[
  \sup\rho \;=\; 2^{-1/(\omega-1)},
\]
and the supremum is never achieved at any finite triple.

\emph{Proof (Theorem~\ref{thm:pattern}):} With $a=2$, group minimums $\{2,\min P_b,\min P_c\}$,
$\mathrm{nd}=\min(\min P_b,\min P_c)$; all other $\omega-2$ primes are distinct
from $\mathrm{nd}$ and hence strictly greater.  Key lemma: $m$ positive integers
all $> n$ have product $> n^m$ (since each $\ge n+1$, product $\ge (n+1)^m > n^m$).
Hence $\rho^{\omega-1}=\mathrm{nd}^{\omega-2}/(2\cdot\prod_{\omega-2\text{ primes}>nd})<
\mathrm{nd}^{\omega-2}/(2\cdot\mathrm{nd}^{\omega-2})=1/2$.
Sharpness: as all $k_1{+}k_2$ primes $\to n$ with $2+b=c$, $\rho^{\omega-1}\to 1/2$. $\square$

Numerically verified for $\omega=3,\ldots,7$ (zero violations).
Specific cases: $(1,1,1)\to 2^{-1/2}$; $(1,1,2),(1,2,1)\to 2^{-1/3}$;
$(1,2,2)\to 2^{-1/4}$.  The type $(1,1,3)$ is \emph{unbounded}
(Theorem~\ref{thm:f11}), confirming the bound depends on partition type.
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection*{General pattern}

The classification for $\omega \in \{3,4,5\}$ exhibits a unified rule:
the non-degenerate ratio $\|\psi_{\text{nd}}\|_\infty / R^{1/(\omega-1)}$ is
\emph{bounded} if and only if there exist two primes from opposite sign-sides
(from $P_a \cup P_b$ vs.\ $P_c$, or from $P_a$ vs.\ $P_b$) that can be
paired with a small $\pm 1$ $\varphi$-entry while the remaining large primes carry
zero weight.  This holds precisely when no single constituent monopolizes the
small primes --- equivalently, when $\max(n_a, n_b, n_c) \le 2$.

\section{Conclusion}

We have proved that for squarefree coprime triples $(a,b,c)$ with $a+b=c$ and
$\omega(abc) \ge 2$, the Pasten lattice satisfies $\det(F(a,b)) < \rad(abc)$,
and hence Minkowski's theorem~\cite{Cassels1959} gives a nonzero vector with
$\|\psi\|_\infty \le \rad(abc)^{1/(\omega-1)}$.

Beyond the determinant bound, we have established a complete classification of
non-degeneracy for all partition types with $\omega \le 5$, proved that all
types with $\omega \ge 6$ are unbounded (Theorem~\ref{thm:f11}), and determined
the exact sharp bounds for every universally-bounded type
(Theorems~\ref{thm:sharp} and~\ref{thm:pattern}).  The central structural
results are: (a) the Universal Divisibility Lemma ($q \mid \psi_q$ for all $q \in P$,
proved by elementary divisibility from the lattice constraint); (b) the $\varphi$-coordinate
change reducing $F(a,b)$ to the simplest integer hyperplane lattice; (c) the optimal
crossing formula (Theorem F10): the minimum non-degenerate norm is the second smallest
of $\{\min P_a, \min P_b, \min P_c\}$, achieved by any cross-group prime pair;
(d) the universal lower bound (Theorem E$_n$): every non-degenerate $\psi$ satisfies
$\|\psi\|_\infty \ge \mathrm{nd}$, proved by a clean F8+constraint+Wronskian argument
and Lean-formalized for $\omega=3,4$ (E10/E11), numerically verified for all types $\omega\le 7$;
(e) a complete table of sharp non-degenerate minimum norms for all partition types
$\omega \in \{3,4,5\}$ (Table in Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp}); (f) the unifying
pattern $\sup_{\text{balanced}}(\omega) = 2^{-1/(\omega-1)}$ for \emph{all} $\omega \ge 3$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:pattern}, F28), with the general key lemma Lean-formalized; and
(g) the general bound $\rho^4 < 1/6$ for \emph{all} type $(2,2,1)$ triples (F29,
Lean-formalized), with $\sup = 6^{-1/4}$ approached by the $a=6$ near-twin-prime subfamily;
and (h) the corrected bound $\rho^4 < 1/2$ for \emph{all} type $(2,1,2)$ triples (F30,
Lean-formalized, correcting F25), with $\sup = 2^{-1/4}$ equal to the $(1,2,2)$ value by
$a \leftrightarrow b$ symmetry; and (i) the Quality-$\rho$ Joint Bound (F31,
Theorem~\ref{thm:f31}): for every non-trivial squarefree triple of any bounded-$\rho$
type, $\operatorname{quality} + \rho^{\omega-1} < 1$, proved by combining quality $< 1/2$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:quality}) with $\rho^{\omega-1} < 1/2$ (Theorems F12--F30);
and (j) the second-minimum and complete spectrum results for type $(1,1,1)$: the
second minimum non-degenerate norm equals $r$ (F32a, Theorem~\ref{thm:f32a}),
and for every twin-prime triple with $q \ge 5$ the full spectrum of achievable
non-degenerate norms is exactly $\{kq:k\ge 1\} \cup \{kr:k\ge 1\}$
(F32b, Theorem~\ref{thm:f32b}), both Lean-formalized; the Frobenius number of the
spectrum is $q^2-2$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:f32c}); and second minimum
$\min(r_2,q,2r_1)$ for type $(1,1,2)$ (F33, Theorem~\ref{thm:f33}), Lean-formalized;
and (k) the non-squarefree upper bound (OB-13B, Theorem~\ref{thm:ob13b}):
$\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \le v_{\max} \cdot R^{1/(\omega^*-1)}$ for all coprime triples,
proved via the cross-group vector construction, with a tighter median form
(Corollary~\ref{cor:ob13b-sharp}), and the within-group construction showing that the
squarefree E$_n$ lower bound fails for non-squarefree triples
(counterexample $(72,11,83)$); (l) the universal lower bound (Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_lb}):
$\mathrm{nd}(a,b) \ge p_2$ (second-smallest prime in $\mathrm{rad}(abc)$) for all coprime
triples, squarefree or not, proved by a single-prime-constraint argument
(Lemma~\ref{lem:single_prime}), tight at $(1,2,3)$; and (m) the exact formula for
$\omega^*=2$ triples (Theorem~\ref{thm:nd_omega2},
Corollary~\ref{cor:nd_omega2_simp}):
$\mathrm{nd}(a,b) = \max(p\cdot w/g,\; q\cdot v/g)$ where $g = \gcd(v,w)$,
verified on all seven $\omega^*=2$ examples up to $c \le 1000$, Lean-formalized;
and (ag) the complete norm spectrum for $\omega^*=2$ triples
(Corollary~\ref{cor:nd_omega2_spectrum}): the achievable norms of non-degenerate
vectors are exactly $\{k \cdot \mathrm{nd}(a,b) : k \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}\}$, so all
successive minima satisfy $\lambda_k = k \cdot \mathrm{nd}$---the lattice is
effectively one-dimensional and its spectrum is a pure arithmetic progression;
and (n) the exact formula for type $(k,1,1)$ non-squarefree triples

``losing'' branch of the $\lambda_2$ competition always yields $\lambda_3$;
verified on $360$ squarefree type-$(1,1,2)$ triples with $c \le 500$ (zero failures, T94b).

The non-degenerate ratio $\|\psi_{\text{nd}}\|/R^{1/(\omega-1)}$ is bounded for exactly
the ``balanced'' partition types (both other constituents non-empty with available small-prime
crossings), and grows without bound for the ``isolated'' types where both non-dominant
constituents are forced-large single primes.  For $\omega \ge 6$, every type is unbounded.
The sharp bounds for the bounded types are now analytically complete (Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp}),
with exact suprema proved for $\omega \in \{3,4,5\}$, the universal formula
$2^{-1/(\omega-1)}$ proved for all balanced single-prime-group types (Theorem~\ref{thm:pattern}),
the general bound $\rho^4 < 6^{-1}$ proved for all type $(2,2,1)$ triples with
$\sup = 6^{-1/4}$ (F29), and the general bound $\rho^4 < 1/2$ proved for all type $(2,1,2)$
triples with $\sup = 2^{-1/4}$ (F30).  Both bounds are Lean-formalized.

We also prove a quality boundary for the squarefree subfamily
(Proposition~\ref{prop:quality}): quality $> 1/2$ if and only if $a = 1$.
Within bounded partition types, quality and $\rho$ are negatively correlated
($r \approx -0.76$ for types $(1,1,2)$ and $(1,2,1)$): the triples
closest to the sharp $\rho$ bound have quality $\to 1/3$, while the highest-quality
non-trivial squarefree triples ($a=2$, $b\to\infty$) have $\rho \to 0$.
This quality-$\rho$ trade-off is a provable structural feature, not a numerical artifact.

\textbf{Honest scope.}
None of these results constitute progress toward the abc conjecture or SDC.
The Minkowski bound is $\eta_R = 1/(\omega-1)$ relative to $\rad(abc)$, whereas
SDC requires $\eta_c < 1$ relative to $c$ for \emph{all} $\varepsilon > 0$ --- an
exponential improvement.  The classification of degenerate vs.\ non-degenerate types
is structural geometry of Pasten's lattice; it does not produce bounds on $c$.
The abc conjecture remains open.

This is a structural result about the geometry of Pasten's lattice construction,
not progress toward abc.  The abc conjecture requires $\|\psi\|_\infty = o(c^\varepsilon)$
for all $\varepsilon>0$ (Pasten's SDC), and the gap between our bound and SDC is
the full depth of the problem.  We have made no use of any abc-equivalent
hypothesis, known abc triples, or the Szpiro conjecture.
